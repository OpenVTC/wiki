# OpenVTC — The Trust Community CLI

*Repo: [github.com/OpenVTC/openvtc](https://github.com/OpenVTC/openvtc)*

OpenVTC is the user-facing tool for participating in [[verifiable-trust-community|Verifiable Trust Communities]]. It's a Rust CLI and TUI (terminal user interface) that orchestrates identity creation, relationship building, and credential exchange — making the complex machinery of decentralized trust accessible to end users.

## What It Does

OpenVTC implements the [[first-person-network|First Person Protocol]] for the "Know Your Developer" use case. From a user's perspective:

1. **Set up your account** — bind the TUI to your [[verifiable-trust-agent|VTA]] (a managed one on the VTA Farm or your own — see [[vti-setup]]) and a Trust Context on it; since June 2026 setup mints *no* persona. Since Dogwood, setup **looks before it writes**: pointing a new install at a context that already holds an account offers to *recover* it from what the VTA holds, rather than silently minting a second set of personas beside the first
2. **Manage your own identity** — the **My Identity** pane (September 2026): mint Persona DIDs ([[did-webvh|did:webvh]], with a chosen or server-allocated path, in a VTA context of your choosing), optionally claim a human-readable **agent name** (`example.com/@alice`), and — one layer up — curate the pool of *facts* (attributes) behind those personas, the *faces* (profiles) that group them, which face each community is shown, and the record of what has actually been disclosed
3. **Connect with people** — send and accept relationship requests over [[trust-spanning-protocol|TSP]] or [[didcomm|DIDComm]]; since Dogwood a new relationship defaults to a **pairwise R-DID**, and the [[relationship-credential|VRC]] it produces is issued under that R-DID rather than the persona
4. **Build your trust network** — exchange [[relationship-credential|Relationship Credentials]], receive [[endorsement-credential|endorsements]], get [[witness-credential|witness attestations]]
5. **Participate in communities** — join VTCs by DID *or* agent name, and by whichever way in the community offers: present a [[invitation-credential|Verifiable Invitation Credential]], **be vetted by existing members** ([[peer-identity-vetting]] — by *named* vetters, or since October 2026 by **hidden vetters**, whose vouching is proven to the community with a zero-knowledge proof that never names them), or send an open request for admin approval. Since 0.5.0 every join ends on a **review page** that lists exactly what the community will receive, and then asks — unticked by default — whether the community may publish your membership in its Trust Registry. Belong to **multiple communities at once**, each under its own persona in its own VTA sub-context; renew a membership (`R`), rescue an approved join whose credential never arrived, see which capabilities each community has enabled and why a membership was rejected or removed, and grant a second device access to one community only
6. **Vet others** — if a community has named you a *vetter* (a revocable `vetter` role credential, a DTG v1 Authority Credential), hand out tickets (QR included), run a guided session as a step-by-step **journey** against an applicant's Vetting Card, and sign `vetted/1` Vetting Statements from the **Vetting desk** — or, where the community hides its vetters, spend an attestation token drawn on a schedule and attest without your DID ever reaching the community
7. **Assert personhood** — answer a community's personhood ceremony over Trust Tasks, matching a spoken eight-character code with the administrator in the room, so the [[personhood-credential|PHC]] claim lands on your [[membership-credential|VMC]]
8. **Work in the community's repositories** — the **Repos** panel (`r` on a community, since 0.4.0) shows the git namespaces a community governs on GitHub or Forgejo ([[community-git-namespaces]]): which repositories you may create, own, maintain or sign for, who else is on each, and drift the community's bridge has reported. Since 0.5.0 it also clones checkouts, sets up [[verifiable-git-infrastructure|did-git-sign]] for the persona *without* `did-git-sign init` or a `pnm` grant, and makes a checkout sign — with the VTA doing the signing over `keys/sign-sshsig`, so the private key never leaves it

Behind the scenes, OpenVTC orchestrates the VTA (for key management, the credential vault, and — increasingly — as the *system of record* for the account), TSP/DIDComm messaging on the TDK's reliable delivery layer (for communication), and Trust-Task documents carried in the binding envelope (for every protocol exchange). Since Eucalyptus-RC-0 it speaks **TSP Rev 3**, forming and persisting the §7.2.2 relationship a VTC requires before any application traffic is accepted. Since 0.4.0 **every Trust Task request it sends is signed as the persona** (the authentication key for acts on one's own standing, the assertionMethod key for claims to be held to later) and **every credential it receives is verified** — issuer proof, validity window, revocation status, fail-closed — before it is stored: "decisions about who said something rest on a proof, not on the sender".

## Components

The workspace is three crates, all `publish = true` since #422 (2026-10-03, with `docs/releasing.md` describing a manual release), though nothing has yet been pushed to crates.io. After sitting at **0.3.1** from 2026-08-16 through `Cypress`, `VTI-Dogwood` and `VTI-Eucalyptus-RC-0` while some 2,400 lines of `[Unreleased]` CHANGELOG accumulated, the version moved twice in the Eucalyptus window: **0.4.0** (tag `v0.4.0`, 2026-10-04, #424 — "the vetted-admission release", folding in everything since 0.3.1) and **0.5.0** (2026-10-05/06, #443 and #481 — "the guided-vetting release"; a minor bump because `openvtc_core::join::JoinPresentation` gained a field; the CHANGELOG dates it 2026-10-06 and there is no `v0.5.0` tag). **`VTI-Eucalyptus` (2026-10-07) ships openvtc 0.5.0.** The coordinated tree-named tags ([[coordinated-releases]]) still mark the stack-wide release; the crate version once again marks the client's own.

### openvtc
The user-facing TUI binary (formerly `openvtc-cli2`). The unsuffixed name is intentional, matching the convention used by uv, ruff, deno, and cargo. Main-menu panels (in menu order, as of 2026-10-07):

- **Communities** — the landing page: memberships with favorites, a **Ctrl+K** switcher, join (`j` — by community DID or agent name; the flow reads the community's requirements from the endpoint it publishes, speaks `join-requests` 0.3 and falls back to 0.2, and lists *every way in* — invitation, vetting, open request — saying which are open to this account and why; a persona or a face can be made without leaving it, and a second persona can apply), **review before sending** and the **Trust registry consent** page (0.5.0), per-community **Capabilities** panel (`c`), the **Repos** panel (`r`, see below), **renew** a membership or **rescue** an approved join whose credential never came (`R`), leave/archive, **delete a finished community's VTA context** (`D`), **per-community device access** (`g`), each row's "wears: *face*" clause (with what the community is allowed to call it) and how the membership is kept apart (own context / shared / top), and a `Reason:` block for rejected or removed memberships. Joins still being vetted are listed; an ended join can be applied for again
- **Inbox** — real-time task processing (auto-handles trust-pongs, relationship finalization, rejections; queues interactive tasks; detail views for inbound/outbound requests, VRCs, pings, informational messages); `a` accepts a relationship request *pairwise*, `p` under the persona DID; a vetting task opens on the journey step where it is taken
- **My Relationships** — list/detail/new-request views, inline alias editing, the pairwise-vs-persona toggle with its consequences spelled out (seeded from the community's `relationshipIdentifierDefault`), trust-ping with RTT latency
- **My Credentials** — Received/Issued tabs plus, since 0.4.0, a **Vetting** tab for every statement a vetter has issued to you; the list leads with *what* each credential is (Membership, Role: vetter, a statement), which persona holds it and a readable validity, with **PQC-SIGNED** and **PCS ZKP** badges where they apply; raw JSON in detail view, clipboard copy, VRC request and removal
- **Vetting** (since September 2026) — two tabs: **Applications** (be vetted: one per community, each opening as a full-width **journey** — Requirements → Face → Vetters → Sessions → Statements → Join — with a step strip, a "What's happening" explainer and only the current step's keys; abandon with `x`) and the **Vetting desk** (vet others: headed by the `vetter` role credential that names you, with Requests / Tickets / Issued sub-views, each request its own journey — Request → Session → Match code → Card → Check → Sign — attest or decline with an optional private reason, withdraw a statement, QR tickets, your vetter profile and the directory). For communities that hide their vetters the desk header shows your **token balance**, `k` gets tokens, `e` requests event-mode tokens for a conference, and `h` opens the **Hidden vetting** view (enrollment, tokens held / usable / spent, live labels, rate and tick length, next window, last refusal)
- **My Identity** (since September 2026) — five tabs: **Personas** (mint with a chosen DID path and context; agent names), **Your facts** (the attribute pool; values fetched only on request; sensitive facts masked and fetched one at a time; purge earlier versions), **Faces** (profiles over the pool, with a cursor to reveal a claim; retire and reinstate a face, read where it has been worn and told; faces made inside a community), **Communities** (which face each community is shown and what it calls it; make a face for a community right there; the **People** a persona has heard from; who the community knows you as), **What has left** (the disclosure record, and the **Formats** a disclosure can take). **Worlds** are offered once a holder has four faces. Personas used to live on the VTA panel; "identity is not a property of the agent that hosts its keys"
- **Settings** — inline editing, config export/import, passphrase protection management, hardware token detection, **Theme** picker with live preview, factory reset (naming the `pnm contexts delete <id>` to run); mediator DID is read-only until a persona exists. The hard-coded "Org DID" row is gone (#459)
- **VTA Service** — "about the agent again": transports the VTA advertises (TSP / DIDComm / REST), the VIC list, key count, backend type, and since 0.5.0 a **Replies** row that notices an admin session whose replies have stopped arriving and shows the rebuild
- **TSP Relationships** (since Eucalyptus-RC-0) — each joined community's TSP Rev 3 §7.2.2 relationship state (None / Pending / Invite received / Bidirectional); a different keyspace, lifecycle and wire from the DIDComm relationships model
- **Logs** — scrollable, transport-attributed, 200-entry durable activity log with copy; listener lines carry the listener id and the speaking persona, and a mediator's scheduled reconnect is one calm line
- **Help/Status**, **Quit**

The **Repos** panel (`r` on an Active community; #377, #379, #384, #391, #425, #477, #480) is the member's side of [[community-git-namespaces]]. It reads `git-ns/view` and shows the community's repositories with the member's strongest right on each (implied rights included), where they may create one, the people on a repository, creation progress and **drift** the bridge reported — which an owner or namespace admin can **revert** (`v`) or **adopt** (`o`) from here. Administrators see a banner for every unratified **break-glass** right. The new-repository form can name co-owners. Since 0.5.0 each repository also shows what is on *this machine*: `c` clones into `<workspace>/<forge>/<owner>/<repo>`, `e`/`E` make a checkout sign or stop, `u` adopts an existing checkout, `s` sets did-git-sign up for the persona (or repairs it), `S` removes it, `f` chooses the **forge account** (a `gh` login, an SSH key, or the git default) a repository clones and pushes with — `F` forks when that account cannot push — and `w` picks the workspace and protocol (following `gh`'s own `git_protocol` by default).

Non-TUI surface: **`openvtc setup`** (its start page now lists the steps it runs; running the `pnm` command is its own step; the setup DID is new each run and the grant command carries `--admin-handoff`); **`openvtc health [--vtc <did>] [--json] [--recoverable] [--allow-private-probes]`** — reports *local storage first* (which credential store, whether this profile's secret is there), then the DID this install authenticates to the VTA as (with the `pnm acl` commands ready to run), whether this install can read its own faces, each community's TSP relationship state, and graded transport probes that dial public HTTPS only and never follow a redirect; **`openvtc theme list|set|import|export`** (see Configuration below); `--monochrome` (0.5.0) draws without color, as `NO_COLOR` does; `--unlock-code-file <PATH|->` replaces the deprecated `--unlock-code` in argv. A `dev-overrides` cargo feature is the *only* build that honors `OPENVTC_VTA_URL` / `OPENVTC_VTA_DID` / `OPENVTC_MEDIATOR_DID`; release builds ignore them and say so. The TUI starts on Windows again since #419.

### openvtc-vetting-pcs
New in the Eucalyptus window (#396, 2026-09-29): "hidden-vetter admission (ZKP) for OpenVTC — the Predicate Credential System behind the vetting flow". It wraps the published `predicate-credential-system` crate (OpenVTC's fork of `etairi/predicate-credential-system`, with the one patch — caller-supplied application contexts — that the integration needed; the vendored copy and its `PROVENANCE.md` are gone) on arkworks BLS12-381, and holds the applicant engine (`applicant.rs`: a fresh key per application, the proof), the vetter engine (`vetter.rs`, `token.rs`: the blind class credential and the attestation-token wallet), the issuer and verifier halves a community runs (`issuer.rs`, `verifier.rs`, `vtc.rs` — the local stand-in the tests use; the real one is in `vtc-service` behind `--features vetting-pcs`), `snapshot.rs` (what the client persists between a request and its answer), `meta.rs`/`scheme.rs` (the public statement metadata that rides beside an attestation, in JCS bytes so both sides build the same context) and `wire.rs` (`extensions.hiddenVetting`, the JSON both repos must agree on). ## Identity Model

OpenVTC's identity model has three layers, the third added in September 2026:

- **Persona / Membership DIDs** — your public identities, one per community membership, created as [[did-webvh|did:webvh]] hosted on the domain of your choice (via the VTA's configured DID host), with a server-allocated mnemonic path or one you type (#324), and optionally claimed by an **agent name** in `alsoKnownAs`. Since #298/#301 each persona's keys and DID are minted **in a VTA sub-context** — by default one per community, so communities no longer share one key hierarchy and are isolated in more than name. A membership row says when the same persona is shown to other communities, because that linkage is the one thing a holder cannot work out from a single row.
- **Relationship DIDs (R-DIDs)** — private `did:peer` identifiers, one per relationship. Since Dogwood (#254/#255) pairwise is the **default**, not the opt-in, and the VRC is issued *under the R-DID* — the durable artifact both parties keep is the worst place to leak a persona. The three handshake messages still route persona-to-persona (the mediator has to route them before a pairwise channel exists); full pairwise operation is a protocol change tracked as VTI #1054. A community may declare `relationshipIdentifierDefault: attributed`, which seeds the form but never gates it (#335).
- **Facts, faces, and what a persona wears** — the VTA's `persona/*` family, one layer above the DID: an *attribute* is a **fact**, a *profile* is a **face** (a named subset of facts), and a persona **wears** a face in a community (`persona/binding`). Since #362 a binding also carries the **label** a community may call the face — the holder's own name for a face is given only to the holder. Since #367 a face can be *composed* for one community where it is asked for, *retired* (kept with its history) and *reinstated*, and its timeline read — made, worn, taken off, told whom which types, values changed; deleting a face says plainly that it "does not un-tell" the parties it disclosed to. A *facet* became a **world** (#378): a named arrangement of faces, offered — not asked for — once a holder has four. The vocabulary is fixed across the console, `pnm`, the mobile agent and this TUI by `design-docs/persona-vocabulary.md`; the spec's words stay on the wire and off the screen. Reaching this layer needs the `persona-holder` capability (`pnm … --admin-holder` at setup), which grants authority over your own identity without making OpenVTC an administrator of every context (VTI #1286).

A name is only ever *displayed* if it forward-resolves and round-trips back to the labeled DID (user alias → verified agent name → truncated DID); the DID, never the name, is what gets persisted. Sensitive facts are masked by the claim-type registry the agent serves (`persona/claim-types/list`) and fetched one at a time on `s` — bulk reads never carry a card number into the process (#340); since #378 a single fact is read by id (`persona/attribute/get`) rather than by listing and filtering, so the agent's audit trail records a decision about one value, not a sweep of the pool.

**Two keys, two jobs** (#386, #390, VTI-KEY-022). Each persona DID publishes an assertionMethod key (`#key-1`) and an authentication key (`#key-2`). Since 0.4.0 every Trust Task request leaves signed: *claims to be held to later* — a join submit, a self-removal, the reciprocal member VMC, a VRC, a Vetting Statement, a git-ns grant — under assertionMethod; *acts on one's own standing* — the personhood challenge and assertion, a capability toggle, a renewal, a vetter profile — under authentication. The VTC's strict proof-purpose rule refuses a proof by a key not listed under the purpose it claims, so getting this wrong is a refusal, not a warning.

**TSP Rev 3 relationships** are a fourth, separate keyspace (Eucalyptus-RC-0, #333): the §7.2.2 relationship this account has *formed* with each community VTC, without which the VTC silently drops application traffic. They are persisted in `ProtectedConfig` so a restart does not forfeit them.

## Configuration

OpenVTC uses a three-tier configuration system:

| Tier | Storage | Contents |
|------|---------|----------|
| **PublicConfig** | JSON on disk | Non-sensitive settings |
| **SecuredConfig** | OS keyring/keychain | Cryptographic secrets |
| **ProtectedConfig** | Encrypted inside PublicConfig | Sensitive data encrypted with unlock code |

Protection modes:
- **Unlock code** — encrypt with a user-provided passphrase (HKDF-SHA256 + random nonce)
- **Plaintext** — no encryption (development only)
- **Hardware token** — encrypt via OpenPGP card (Nitrokey/YubiKey)

Multiple profiles are supported via the `OPENVTC_CONFIG_PROFILE` environment variable or `-p` flag.

**Where the secrets live (Dogwood, #246).** The OS credential store is now chosen by `vta_sdk::keyring_init::install_default_store` — the same call `pnm-cli` makes — so `openvtc` and `pnm` keep secrets in the same place: Apple Keychain, Windows Credential Manager, or DBus Secret Service. On Linux OpenVTC had been the only tool using the kernel `keyutils` keyring, which is RAM-only and had been quietly destroying accounts on reboot. If the store cannot be opened OpenVTC **fails closed** rather than downgrading; headless machines opt into an encrypted file store with `OPENVTC_SECURE_STORE=file` (`~/.config/openvtc/secrets/`, mode 0600, refuses to hold an unencrypted profile). The secured envelope is written as one line, because gnome-keyring corrupted its whole collection on a pretty-printed one (#269). `ProtectedConfig` is now encrypted under a **random per-profile key** held in `SecuredConfig`, not under a derivation of the admin credential (D12) — so the credential can rotate or be re-issued to a recovering install without making local state undecryptable. Startup failures each get their own diagnosis (`~/.config/openvtc/last-startup-failure.txt`).

**The VTA as system of record.** `docs/design/vta-authoritative-state.md` (DRAFT v6) sets the direction: keys, identities and credentials live at the VTA; local config is a cache that can be rebuilt. The rebuild path (#248–#251) reconstructs personas (re-establishing DID ↔ key correspondence with the VTA's own `select_secret_kid` rule) and memberships (from VMCs now stored in the VTA credential vault via `vault/credentials/receive`), and setup offers it when a context is already occupied. What is still missing is an application-state store on the VTA (ask E2, *blocking*) for the metadata that is neither key nor credential.

**Themes (`tui.toml`).** Since #299/#300 the TUI is drawn in any theme: built-in (including High Contrast and Colourblind Safe pairs measured against WCAG), your own under `~/.config/openvtc/themes`, Omarchy's (followed live), or imported from base16/base24, Alacritty, Kitty, Ghostty, or any Neovim colorscheme. `auto` — the default since 0.5.0 (#457) — follows the terminal background (OSC 11 → `COLORFGBG` → dark); a terminal that does not declare 24-bit color in `COLORTERM` is drawn with the nearest of the 256 indexed colors, which is what made macOS Terminal.app readable; `NO_COLOR` and `--monochrome` draw without color, keeping roles apart with bold, underline and reverse (#460); `OPENVTC_THEME` overrides for one session; `openvtc theme export` writes a theme back out for other tools. The choice belongs to you rather than to a profile (a per-profile remembered choice was scoped in #336).

**No built-in mediator or organization.** Until 0.5.0 setup stamped a fixed organization DID on `fpp.storm.ws` into every account and a persona minted against a VTA that advertised no DIDComm mediator fell back to a built-in public one on the same host. Both are gone (#459): the mediator comes from your VTA's DID document, and create-persona and the join flow refuse — before minting anything — when the VTA advertises none.

**Repositories (`git_workspace`, `docs/forge-accounts.md`).** The Repos panel keeps checkouts under a workspace root (`<root>/<forge-host>/<owner>/<repo>`, or a path you gave), stores only an explicit protocol choice (`clone_protocol`) and, per community-and-forge or per repository, a *reference* to the forge account git should use — a `gh` login or an SSH key path, never a token. did-git-sign identities OpenVTC provisions are stored as did-git-sign's own **named profiles**, in the same OS credential store OpenVTC uses (which is why VGI 0.18.2 had to switch did-git-sign's Linux store from the kernel keyring to Secret Service, #148).

#### Hidden vetters: the Predicate Credential System (#396, #433–#434, #442–#443, #449, #452–#456, #461–#468, #471–#472; 09-22 → 10-06)

The V0 spec listed "k-of-n proofs over hidden vetters" as V2. It shipped in V0's own window. **`docs/design/vetting-hidden-vetters-pcs.md`** (2026-09-22, with a same-day second-pass review and dated addenda through October) sets the goal: *Bob proves to a VTC that k distinct, currently eligible vetters of that community vetted him, without the VTC — or anyone — learning which.* The construction is the **Predicate Credential System** (PCS, `etairi/predicate-credential-system`, BLS12-381): a *helper* issues blind class credentials; a credentialed user *attests* for another's identifier; the applicant *proves* it holds k attestations from pairwise-distinct credentialed users, none of whom is itself. Each attestation carries a tag that is deterministic per (vetter, applicant) — so the same vetter twice is caught — but unlinkable across applicants and to the vetter, even for the helper. An attestation is 240 bytes and ~3 ms; a proof at k = 5 is 1.4 kB and ~12 ms to make or check. It is a research artifact, unaudited; OpenVTC forked it, added the one change the integration needed (caller-supplied application contexts), and **published it to crates.io** so the vendored copy could go (#396, 09-29).

What makes it fit is the role mapping: `vtc-service` is the helper, with keys *derived* from the community's credential signer rather than stored; the vetter's class credential is issued when the `vetter` role is granted; and the VTC emits the *same* `VettingFacts` shape with tags where issuer DIDs used to be, so counting, method floors, independence caps and `join.rego` are untouched. Accountability is kept by **attestation tokens on an unconditional drip** — a vetter draws on a schedule whether or not they vetted anyone, "because a fetch that happened only when someone was busy would announce that they were busy" — with ticks that are windows of time from a label's start (#452, trust-tasks #734) and an **event mode** (`e`) for conferences. The ceremony up to the human check is unchanged; only what the vetter hands back, and what the VTC verifies, differ. The community decides the mode in its manifest's `vetting.ext`; the client needs no flag, and `vtc-service` must be built `--features vetting-pcs`. **`docs/design/hidden-vetting-live-run.md`** walks a real community through it: register a criterion, `POST /v1/vetting/hidden`, name vetters, each vetter's client enrolls and draws (`m`), the applicant mints a key for this application and no other, a ticket, a session, a card, one proof over three attestations — and afterwards the community can show "three distinct vetters, their methods, their claims — and three tags where three DIDs would be".

The week of 0.5.0 was the first time it met real clocks and real mediators, and the fixes are the story: the join **fetches its own challenge** (`vtc/vetting/pcs-challenge/0.1`, 15 s, Esc to leave; #442, #443) and a join refused over its challenge ends with the reason instead of sitting Pending (#454); a vetter **learns that a community turned PCS on** — the re-read and enrollment used to run only on `m` from an *application*, which a vetter-only member never has — and never signs a named statement for it (#449); the four `vtc/vetting/...#response` types were **never claimed by the inbound router**, so every enrollment the community issued was dropped and every retry refused `alreadyEnrolled` (#456); a token balance on the desk, and no ticket you cannot honor (#456); tickets follow the *live* mode, `k` gets tokens in one key, the enrollment blinding is saved before the request so an answer survives a restart (#462); a lost enrollment is asked for once more (#466, VTI #1972); the join page shows a proof as ready rather than "0 statements" (#467); when a community changes its published parameters the criterion digest moves and earlier attestations stop counting — the checklist now says so and the join stops before submitting (#468); draws follow the community's *current* rate (#468); a vetter follows the request — a PCS attestation for one that carries an identifier, a named statement otherwise — and a new application defaults to hidden vetting where the community offers it (#471); the mode is read once the listener is up, not minutes later (#472).

#### DTG Credentials v1 and the proof-first security pass (#371–#372, #380–#392, #397–#398, #403, #406–#408, #411; 09-23 → 10-02)

- **Sign what the specs say to sign** (#371). `join-requests/{submit,status}`, `members/{self-remove,vmc}` and `members/personhood/assert` went out unsigned, relying on the authcrypt or TSP sender. Their specs declare `proof` REQUIRED, and VTI #1659 had had to add `require_declared_proof = false` to the VTC *because of this client*. "Transport attribution is not the same claim: an authcrypt sender says who handed the document over, the proof says who wrote it." One `trust_task_doc::build_signed_value` now signs every one of them.
- **Keyring VTI-42** (#372). The Berkman Klein Center's **Keyring** phone wallet, interoperating against the same VTCs, found that OpenVTC still typed several messages with the task URI instead of the DIDComm binding envelope; VTI #1687 then removed the VTC's task-typed arms, and this PR moves the manifest request, the vetter directory/profile/resend, revoke-statement and both personhood legs into the envelope, and opens the envelope *before* routing inbound.
- **Decisions rest on a proof, not the sender** (#380–#392). A credential delivered over `credential-exchange/issue` was stored after checking only that its issuer matched the DIDComm sender; now its Data Integrity proofs (eddsa-jcs-2022 and mldsa44-jcs-2024) must verify with an assertionMethod key of the issuer's own DID, its validity window must hold and its status list must be *reachable and verifiable* — fail closed, with the list read and checked locally (`status_list.rs`), proof sets included. An **approved status or allow verdict no longer activates a membership**: the join is acknowledged, and Active — with the reciprocal VMC sent — only when the community's credential arrives and verifies. Proofs are required on relationship DIDs, removal notices and community answers (#381); capability and git-ns replies, invitations and join failures are authenticated (#383); pushed credential-exchange documents are read and `request-vmc` answered only when signed (#392); **every Trust Task request is signed as the persona** (#386), with the authentication key for operational acts (#390, trust-tasks 0.23 making `personhood/challenge`, `vetters/profile` and `vetters/resend` proof-REQUIRED). Network-bound checks run off the UI loop (#387).
- **DTG Credentials v1** (#397, 10-01, breaking; dtg-credentials 0.12 → 0.13, vta-sdk 0.57). Every DTG credential the client issues, receives, verifies or stores carries the v1 context `https://registry.trustoverip.org/dtg/context/v1` (the `firstperson.network` context refused, no alias), a top-level **`issuerScope`** (`directed` for the persona DID a membership names, `pairwise` for a relationship DID, `public` for a VIC or the community's own VMC) and exactly one concrete type. The vetter's Vetting Statement is a `StatementCredential` under the registry predicate **`vetted/1`**, citing the signed `vetting/session` document by `taskContext` and `taskDigestMultibase`; witness statements are **`witnessed/1`**; the vetter role is an **Authority Credential** (`role:vetter`) rather than a `CommunityRole`. Credentials stored in a pre-v1 shape are **set aside on load** and listed as retired — hence **membership renewal** (`vtc/members/renew/0.1`, `R`, #398), which asks the community to re-issue the VMC and role credential and verifies both before replacing the old ones. A community may issue `vetted/1` for *itself* (vta-sdk 0.59, #403), and OpenVTC keeps that and offers it as personhood evidence (#408). See [[dtg-credential-spec]] and [[dtg-credentials]].
- **A join that was approved but whose credential never arrived** can be rescued by hand (`R`, #406) and, since #411, asks automatically: the status poll carries `resendCredentials` once the community has reported `credentialsDelivered: false` (trust-tasks #709, VTI #1900), at most six times per record per process.

#### The community's repositories: the Repos panel (#377, #379, #382–#384, #391, #425, #474–#480; 09-24 → 10-06)

- **The member side of git namespaces** (#377, 09-24; trust-tasks 0.22.3 with the `git-ns` feature). `openvtc_core::git_ns` sends the eight member-facing `git-ns/*` Trust Tasks — view, repo/create, right/grant, right/revoke, repo/transfer, repo/archive, account/link, account/link-status — in the generated types (so an unqualified resource or a DID without a method fails *before* it is sent, naming the field), each signed by the persona and carried in the binding envelope. A `trust-task-error` becomes a `Refusal` whose `explain()` gives each declared code the fix it implies: `lastOwner` says to add another owner first, `policyDenied` keeps the VTC's reason. The panel shows the member's repositories with their strongest right, where they may create, the people on a repository, creation progress and drift. **Drift** can be answered from the panel (#379, `git-ns/drift/resolve`): `v` reverts to what the rights call for, `o` adopts a forge-side role as the right it projects, each armed and classed as the revocation or grant it amounts to. **Break-glass** rights are shown to every administrator they concern, with the `cnm` commands to ratify or revoke (#384, `git-ns/view` 0.4). **`repo/create` 0.3** closed a hole (#391): `git.ns.admin` implies `git.repo.create`, and 0.2 made every creator the owner — so a namespace admin could make themselves owner and forge admin of any repository, alone; now the requester becomes owner only when their create right is an *explicit* grant, and a refused elevated self-grant (`git-ns:selfGrantNotAllowed`) is explained as separation of duties. - **Checkouts and signing** (0.5.0, #425, 10-04). `git_workspace` keeps a checkout at `<root>/<forge>/<owner>/<repo>`, inspects what git reports (origin, branch, ahead/behind, the signing settings in effect, whether `HEAD` is signed and carries a `Signed-by-DID:` claim) and clones without ever prompting. `git_signing` sets did-git-sign up for a persona **without `did-git-sign init` or a `pnm` grant**: openvtc mints a `did:key`, grants it — through its own admin session — `admin` of the persona's *own* sub-context narrowed to one capability, proves the grant before storing anything, revokes it if the proof fails, stores the identity as a named did-git-sign profile with the library's own calls, and runs `did-git-sign enable --profile` in each checkout. ### Dogwood (2026-08-30) → Dogwood-R1 (08-31) → Eucalyptus-RC-0 (2026-09-17): the VTA as system of record, vetted admission, and the identity pane

The workspace stays at 0.3.1 throughout — no `vX.Y.Z` tag, no GitHub Release for Dogwood, ~960 lines of `[Unreleased]` CHANGELOG. Two design specs frame the window. **`docs/design/vta-authoritative-state.md`** (DRAFT v6) starts from *"My laptop died. I install OpenVTC on a new machine, point it at my VTA and my Trust Context, and it offers to recover"* — and finds that the admin credential was doing two irreconcilable jobs, that concurrent instances break the messaging layer *today*, and that the VTA needs an application-state store (E2, blocking, still open). **`docs/design/vetting-process.md`** (DRAFT v3, V0 target 5 Oct 2026) replaces the Linux kernel maintainers' PGP web of trust with peer identity vetting: an applicant builds a signed **Vetting Card** from a persona face, presents it to several **vetters** who check it against a passport in person or on video and issue **Vetting Statements**, and submits them to the VTC, which admits under policy. Most of that protocol lands in the ToIP Trust Task specs and VTI; OpenVTC is where both halves — applicant and vetter — become a screen.

#### Dogwood — recoverable installs, honest storage, and the membership pair finally landing (#238–#272)

*Cypress..VTI-Dogwood: 41 commits, 80 files, +18.6k / −2.6k.* A "silent" release by design: almost everything here is a bug that had been making the client lie, and the fix is that it now tells the truth.

- **Storage that survives a reboot** (#246). On Linux OpenVTC had registered the kernel `keyutils` keyring — documented as RAM-only — as the *sole* home of a profile's seed and credential bundle; a reboot, a logout, or three days of the persistent keyring's default expiry destroyed the account, surfacing as `Couldn't find openvtc secured configuration`. Store selection now delegates to the SDK's `install_default_store`, fails closed instead of downgrading, and offers `OPENVTC_SECURE_STORE=file` for headless machines. In the same PR: `Config::save` writes secrets *before* the config file so an interrupted save leaves a persona absent rather than half-written; a half-written persona no longer fails the whole load (`LoadIntegrity`, acknowledged on the startup screen, "nothing has been deleted"); every startup failure gets its own diagnosis instead of "check your network"; and `openvtc health` reports local storage first, even when no account loads.
- **Break the credential ⇄ encryption coupling (D12).** `ProtectedConfig` was keyed on the admin credential's private key — an *authorization grant* meant to rotate, doing duty as a *data-at-rest key* that must not. The profile now carries its own random key; load tries three keys newest-first and re-keys on the next save, crash-safely. Config records gain a flattened `extra` so an older build round-trips a newer one's fields (D19).
- **Setup looks before it writes; recovery is a branch in setup** (#247–#251). Pointing a fresh install at an occupied Trust Context used to mint a second set of personas beside the first. Setup now probes (personas, sub-contexts — counted, never gestured at; "could not tell" kept distinct from "empty"), offers *use a different context* first, *continue* on a deliberate `C`, and never offers delete. `openvtc_core::rebuild` reconstructs an account from what the VTA holds: DID ↔ key correspondence via the VTA's own `select_secret_kid`; memberships rebuilt Active from their VMCs, each carrying its own proof. Running it live found that VMCs had never been stored at the VTA at all, that `cred_vault_query({})` is refused by design (the vault is non-enumerable) and both callers had read the refusal as "zero", and that `query` returns body-free descriptors — so a `credential_sync` on connect now pushes every membership credential through `vault/credentials/receive` (#251).
- **Privacy defaults flipped** (#254, #255 — *breaking on the wire*). A new relationship mints a pairwise R-DID unless turned off; `a` accepts pairwise, `p` under the persona. Then the half that mattered: the VRC is **issued under the R-DID**, signed with its key — previously `issuer = persona_did`, so the durable, publishable credential led straight back to the persona. This needed VTI #1061 to stop pinning a published VRC's issuer to the session DID; `vet_vrc_issued` gate 2 is now tighter *and* more private (issuer must be the sender's DID in *this* relationship). A pasted VIC must now carry DTG's common structure — the `firstperson.network/credentials/dtg/v1` context and `DTGCredential` type — or it is refused at ingest (#256, breaking; the fixtures had encoded the implementation's belief rather than the spec's).
- **Personhood over Trust Tasks** (#257). The VTC's `challenge`/`assert` verbs were REST-only; a client that talks DIDComm/TSP and holds no bearer token could not take part. Both now ride the Trust Task document path. `match_code` derives eight Crockford-base32 characters (no I/L/O/U) from the challenge UUID — the thing two people in a room actually say to each other; `5CY1-GZEE` is pinned on both sides so a drift reads as "software disagrees" rather than "wrong ceremony". The challenge is written twice, to `proof.challenge` (what the spec names) and top-level `nonce` (what the Data Integrity signature actually covers).
- **The membership pair had never once landed** (#259–#267).
