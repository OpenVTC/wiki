# keyring-wallet — the Keyring phone app

*Repo: [github.com/berkmancenter/keyring-wallet](https://github.com/berkmancenter/keyring-wallet) — the app and, since 2026-11-22, its framework too: the core packages that lived in the [[keyring-bifold]] submodule were folded into this repository with their history (now `packages/*`). Developed at the Applied Social Media Lab of Harvard's Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society. Apache-2.0. iOS and Android, React Native 0.81.*

Every other client in this wiki assumes a computer: the [[openvtc|OpenVTC TUI]] wants a terminal, the [[vta-browser-plugin|VTA Wallet]] wants Chrome, `pnm` wants a shell. **Keyring is the phone.** It began life outside the OpenVTC ecosystem altogether, as a fork of the OpenWallet Foundation's Bifold wallet (itself the framework under British Columbia's BC Wallet), carrying the Berkman team's own contribution to decentralized trust: peer-to-peer **Verifiable Relationship Credentials** exchanged between two people who met, witnessed by a third party, with the phone's Secure Enclave or StrongBox vouching that a real device and a real person signed. The team drafted the first DTG credential specification with the ToIP working group ([[dtg-credential-spec]]), and the witnessed exchange Keyring ships is the one that spec describes. Since July 2026 the project has turned toward the OpenVTC stack deliberately, as its plan puts it, "contributor, not consumer": it recast its exchange onto Trust Tasks and TSP, linked the phone to a cloud [[verifiable-trust-agent|Verifiable Trust Agent]], and — in a burst of about 1,200 commits across the two repos in September and early October — became the second client after the TUI to carry the **full [[peer-identity-vetting|peer identity vetting]] ceremony, both seats**, on a device a person already owns.

Between Eucalyptus and Fig (October–November 2026, roughly 2,200 more commits) it stopped being only a vetting client and became a **community app**: private (hidden) vetting from the phone, community [[data-rooms|rooms]] as group chats, one-to-one chat between contacts, community polls with blind ballots, a member directory, sign-in to a community's website and to the VTA Farm from the phone, [[trust-spanning-protocol|TSP 1.0]], and joining communities whose DID is a `did:webs`.

The posture matters as much as the code. Keyring is *not* in the `VTI-Eucalyptus` or `VTI-Fig` tag sets; it is an independent downstream that pins upstream by commit, measures it in a lab it can rebuild, and files numbered findings back. Several OpenVTC and VTI fixes in September and October cite "Keyring VTI-NN" in their titles. That feedback loop — a second, independent implementation testing the first — is the thing Trust Task specifications formally need to leave draft status, and Keyring's plan says so explicitly.

## What It Does

**The witnessed relationship exchange — the flagship.** Two phones connect, each issues the other a VRC under a pairwise relationship DID (the Relationship Credential Exchange protocol, RCE v3; `did:peer` R-DIDs, R-Card profile data with an optional photo), and an optional **witness server** attests that the exchange happened in one session by issuing a Verifiable Witness Credential to each side without seeing their private claims ([[witnessed-vrc-exchange]], [[witness-credential]]). "Secure Exchange" adds **hardware attestation**: a P-256 key in the iOS Secure Enclave (App Attest) or Android StrongBox/TEE signs the credential after Face ID, fingerprint or device passcode, and the attestation chain is embedded as W3C evidence and verified natively on the receiving phone — one OS prompt per exchange since 0.2.0. **Locality** adds co-presence: the phone advertises over BLE as a peripheral, the witness's sensor writes a nonce and reads back a transcript signed by the same attestation key, and the proof rides inside the VWC (Android proven live on devices, iOS type-checked only, both shipped behind the witness's opt-in). Credentials are VC 2.0 with `eddsa-rdfc-2022` Data Integrity proofs; everything else a Bifold wallet does — AnonCreds, W3C VCs, Indy VDR, OpenID4VC — is still there through Credo-TS.

**Three transports side by side.** The legacy exchange rides DIDComm v1 over an Aries-style mediator; since 0.2.0 the same documents can ride **DIDComm v2** (Credo's unreleased v2 from PR #2704, consumed as a pinned `0.7.1-pr-2704` snapshot) and **TSP** ([[trust-spanning-protocol]]: Rev 3 from 2026-09-21, **TSP 1.0** read and packed beside it since 10-22, bifold #492, chosen per upstream peer from 10-23) as a third carriage, with HPKE-Auth on `@noble` so no WebCrypto is needed on Hermes. The team's noble HPKE backend was contributed upstream and became the TSP wire layer of `@openvtc/vti-tsp-js` 0.2.0 at Cypress. The phone↔VTA leg and the phone↔community leg are both Trust Task documents in the published `tsp/0.1` or DIDComm binding envelopes, signed with `eddsa-jcs-2022` document proofs. Keyring does *not* depend on `@openvtc/pnm-core` — it treats that package as a reference and implements the VTA client in its own `@bifold/trust-tasks` and `@bifold/core` (see [[keyring-bifold]]) — but it does take the TSP wire layer from `@openvtc/vti-tsp-js`: the app's TSP oracle is still a vendored 0.3.0 (Rev 3) build with a one-file Hermes patch, while the TSP 1.0 codec is checked against the 1.0 vectors that ship with `vti-tsp-js` 0.4.0 (plugin #374, pinned 10-23), and the vendored build goes once the Rev 3 reader is removed. Since 11-20 (#951) DIDComm v2 and TSP carriage are **on by default**, and Keyring picks the carriage and invitation version per peer (bifold #1162).

**A mailbox per phone.** Wallet-to-wallet messages (relationship exchange, chat) used to depend on Aries-style Coordinate Mediation. In November each phone got **one DIDComm v2 mailbox** on a mediator the team deploys from the new standalone `affinidi-messaging-mediator` (pinned at v0.53.0, `deploy/keyring-messaging/mailbox/`, #961): the phone sets its own forwarding right and wake handle on it, forwards only to peers it has resolved, and after a send asks the recipient's mediator to wake the other phone (bifold #1163–#1170). Retiring Coordinate Mediation 2.0 started on 11-20 (#1166, step 1 of 3); the mailbox is on by default under a new saved key since 11-24 (#996), and CI refuses a store build that would ship with it off (#967).

## Keyring as a PNM: the phone over a cloud agent

In the vocabulary of [[vta-topology]], Keyring is a **Personal Network Manager**: a thin surface over an always-on cloud VTA — on the [[vtafarm|VTA Farm]] or self-hosted — that holds the person's keys and answers while the phone sleeps. The phone's own identity is a software key in the wallet; everything community-facing is a **persona** the agent mints and whose keys the phone borrows into memory only while it uses them. The screens never say "VTA" or show a DID: the agent is "your agent", identities are "the code the community sees for you", and every DID sits behind "Show the code".

- **Link.** Scan the agent's enrollment QR or paste its address; the phone signs in with a temporary key, is granted a seat on the agent's access list, and swaps onto a long-lived key — a reducer-driven state machine (`notLinked → confirming → submitting → awaitingGrant → linking → linked`, with `offline`, `reconnecting`, `revoked` and `gone` as sub-states) so leaving and returning to the screen shows the same step. From 10-01 the Farm's **automatic connection** QR (`{vta_did, callback_url}`) collapsed the whole thing into one scan; since 10-13 that Farm-specific QR is replaced by the standard VTI **`vta-claim` trigger link**: the phone scans an agent host's setup code, sees who it claims to be from (marked unverified until checked), confirms a short comparison code, and sends signed `provision/vta/claim/0.1` / `redeem/0.1` Trust Tasks (bifold, 10-13; [[vtafarm-api]] 0.13.0). The Farm can hand out **pre-provisioned agents** this way at an event or from its public sign-up page.
- **Sign in to the Farm.** A claimed agent keeps its Farm and claim key (#794), so the person can later **sign in to the VTA Farm portal with the phone** — the Farm shows an `auth/oob` QR, the phone picks one of the agent's identities and approves (#797, 10-30; the Farm side is vtafarm 0.17.0). No passkey on the laptop is needed for it.
- **Own.** "Create my agent" (09-25 → 10-01) lets a person with no computer create an agent on a hosting service's website and claim it from the phone: the phone shows the owner code for the Farm's "Admin DID" box, then connects. Owner acts — adding a backup device, removing one — always ask for Face ID again.
- **Devices.** A new phone is a new device (`device/register` 0.2): a devices list with names and last-seen, a one-time offer after a new phone links (keep or remove the old ones), **two phones finishing a link between themselves** (09-28), heartbeats, and a wipe that the agent refuses at login from then on.
- **Several agents.** A row of agent chips across the top of *Your agent*, each with its own count of waiting requests, "Add" at the end; a Wallet card says which agent holds it, cards of an unlinked agent are hidden, and the app looks at the other agents' inboxes when it comes to the foreground.
- **Approvals and consent.** The phone is the agent's approver: a pushed consent request shows the **match code** and what approving would do, and is answered with a signed decision; the agent's `requireStepUp` is answered from the phone with a DID-signed `auth/step-up/approve-response`; **"Ask me before…"** (10-04) sets consent rules from the phone for tasks the phone itself never sends (contexts, keys, ACL changes), lists rules set elsewhere read-only, and warns when one would trap the phone behind its own approval.
- **Push.** Signal-style contentless wake pushes from OpenVTC's push gateway, one wake channel per agent, no Firebase initialized before the person opts in, a notification the phone composes itself (generic text while locked). The gateway image is deployed by this repo's own workflow for its test stack.

## Joining a community from the phone

"I want to join a community" starts from a scanned or pasted `keyring://vti/community` link (or a vetter's ticket, or an invitation), reads the community's **join manifest** (0.2 and 0.3 — a 0.3 criterion states its own admission, `automatic` or `review`, and the screen says a way in admits only when it is automatic), makes the person's identity for that community with one Face ID, and then offers every way in: an invitation from the admin, vetting by a member, a credential the community asks for, or agreement alone. "I was invited" hands the admin the identity to invite (an invitation names one DID, and there is no invitation by reference yet) and redeems the console's QR or DIDComm offer; the join itself presents the [[invitation-credential|invitation]], the vetting statements or both, and on `allow` keeps the [[membership-credential|membership card]], role cards and any vetter grant as Wallet cards. Membership is read back the same way the community means it: removal notices, status lists, `requestAlreadyOpen`, withdraw and supplement, leaving. A community on another mediator is asked over DIDComm from the first message (a TSP Rev 3 relationship did not cross two mediators until the fix in VTI 0.41 / messaging-sdk 0.26.27 — Keyring's VTI-41 — and the Farm's mediator must still be told to admit the relayed hop).

## The vetting flow, on a phone

This is the part the TUI and Keyring now share end to end, and the reason the two were demonstrated side by side for the kernel community in Prague. The ceremony is the one [[peer-identity-vetting]] describes: a **ticket** gates the vetter, a **Vetting Card** carries the applicant's face and legal name, a spoken **match code** binds the person in the room to the client that controls the join DID, and a **Vetting Statement** is what the community counts.

One screen, *Vetting*, seats the person according to what their persona holds: a live `CommunityRole: vetter` grant puts them at the **vetting desk** (its own title, a differently colored header); otherwise it is **their application**. Each step has one filled button, and each step is one screen.

**The applicant.** *Start my application* reads the requirements ("This community needs 2 vetters to confirm your name — 1 in person"), then asks for a face photo and the legal name as on the ID ("the name on your profile may be different" — the card comes from a profile face, as in the TUI). *Ask a vetter*: scan or paste the vetter's ticket; a ticket for another community is refused with a warning that using it would show your identity there. The request goes to the vetter as a signed `vetting/request/0.1` carrying the ticket; the acceptance comes back with the vetter's **eligibility presentation**, which the phone verifies against the manifest's `eligibleVetters.role` before telling the person the statement will count. When the vetter opens the session both phones show the match code ("Read this code aloud — both screens must show the same one"); the applicant confirms it matches, the Vetting Card is sent inside the session's `#response`, and the statement arrives as a signed Trust Task document, checked against the card and the session digest before it is kept. A checklist counts what still is needed by method, discounts statements whose vetter's grant did not cover the moment of signing (checked against the community's status list), caps statements from declared relationships at what the community counts, and lights *Apply* when the criteria are met.

**The vetter's desk.** *New ticket* cuts an eight-character Crockford code with a QR and a link ("Read this code to the person, or let them scan or paste this link"), good for a stated number of uses and a time. Requests arrive under *Requests*; *Open session* sends `vetting/session/0.1` and shows the match code; *Codes match* waits for the card; *Their card* shows the face, the name and the identity code the community sees — the same code the applicant's phone shows under "Your identity in this community", so the two can be compared; **"I checked them and their ID — attest"** issues the statement, and *Statement issued* shows the confirmed name. A vetter can publish a **vetter profile** so applicants can find them in the community's directory, and a desk whose grant was revoked still opens, with the reason it will not sign. Tickets are client-local in V0, exactly as the reference client keeps them.

**What the statement is.** Until 09-30 a Keyring vetter wrote the WD02 `IdentityVetting` endorsement; the community's own in-person identity check is kept as evidence rather than as a vetter's statement. Since the DTG Credentials v1 cutover upstream (VTI 0.47 and OpenVTC `e165bd55`, which refuse the old shape with no dual acceptance), Keyring **reads both shapes always and writes the one the community counts**: the `vetted/1` `StatementCredential` ([[statement-credential]]) with `@context` exactly [VCDM 2.0, registry v1], `taskContext` and `taskDigestMultibase` binding it to the session document, and only the registry's `vetted/1` members in the subject. Since 10-01 it writes v1 by default where a community asks for it; a developer flag forces either. Since 11-24 a community grant that declares `issuerScope: directed` is read as a public one is (#999), following the spec's Working Draft 0.7.0 ([[correlation-scope]]).

**Private vetting.** What the Eucalyptus page listed as "planned" landed on 10-14 and 10-15 in about fifty commits: [[peer-identity-vetting|hidden vetting]], where the applicant proves that enough eligible vetters vouched without naming any. The applicant's five and the vetter's seven `vta/pcs/*` calls run **on the person's agent**, matching OpenVTC's move of the PCS key off the device (openvtc #487), so the phone holds no PCS key. The applicant asks the community for the challenge the proof binds and submits a join carrying the proof and no statements. The vetter's desk marks private requests and can vouch or decline without naming the applicant. Join offers private vetting only when the build carries it, and asks the agent only after a tap. Testers got it as "Private vetting" in builds 242–265 (10-09 → 10-21), alongside a community's **directory statement** shown before a join request is sent (manifest 0.4, 10-18), and a notice when a community ends your vetter role (#910).

**Judged by upstream, not by itself.** Three times Keyring's producer and Keyring's consumer agreed with each other and not with upstream (the card digest, the statement, the eligibility VP). The rule since 09-25: every document Keyring signs or checks is judged in CI by upstream's own Rust (`card-verify`, built from `vta-sdk` at the VTI pin, plus a second clone at `vta-service-v0.47.0` so VTI's SDK judges the v1 statement), and an **interop harness** drives Keyring and the real OpenVTC TUI in lockstep in both directions — Keyring vetter / TUI applicant and the reverse — reading assertions from the TUI's state and the community's records. The pinned TUI moved from `v0.4.0` to OpenVTC at the `VTI-Eucalyptus` tag (`2a4fa2b`, 0.5.0, which carries the VTI-67 `may_claim` fix), and `card-verify` now builds from `vta-service-v0.84.0` (vta-sdk 0.91.0; pinned 11-26 as "what every Farm VTA runs"). The gate's test community lives on the Farm.

**How it compares with the TUI.** Same wire, same ceremony, same V0 limits (client-local tickets, no automated document check), and since October both clients run hidden vetting through the agent. What differs is the medium: the phone's camera scans the ticket and the agent's QR, Face ID stands in for the TUI's key handling, the two seats are one screen rather than two pages, identities are never shown as DIDs, and the applicant's identity is minted *before* the ticket because the desk checks the ticket against it — "the same order as the OpenVTC TUI: the community, its requirements, the identity it will know you as, then the application," as the join screen's own comment says.

## Community life on the phone (October–November 2026)

Once a person could get into a community from the phone, the next question was what they would do there. Between Eucalyptus and Fig, Keyring answered with the surfaces people expect from a messenger, and each one runs on the community's own [[trust-tasks|Trust Tasks]] through the person's agent:

- **Rooms.** A community's [[data-rooms|data rooms]] arrived in late October (R0–R6, bifold #595–#644, 10-26 → 10-28) and went to testers in **build 276** (10-29, #785). Rooms work as group conversations with the newest post at the bottom. They offer posts, titled notes, files up to 25 MB, a room's Files library and members shown by their directory names, and an offer-and-consent screen for being let in. November made them feel live. A room on screen is watched rather than polled every 30 seconds (#1000), it opens on a **kept copy** of its newest posts before the listing answers (the message cache, bifold #1130–#1147, on by default from 279.4), and **read markers are kept by the person's agent**, so all their devices agree on what is unread (11-25).
- **Chat.** One-to-one chat between contacts is a new `keyring/chat/1.0` message announced with RCE 6 (bifold #525–#577, 10-24 → 10-25). It has reactions, replies, an emoji picker, inline photos checked against their bytes before anything decodes them, disappearing messages with a bound on what a peer may set, and a "Keep messages" setting. A single **Messages** list holds both chats and rooms (L2–L5 and R1, #639–#801).
- **Contacts and cards.** Contacts are grouped by person, not by relationship DID. "Same person?" merges two entries, confirmed by the other person's phone over `contacts/continuity-check` (#505–#535). A relationship can be upgraded in place by signing again or adding a witness (#471), and an invitation can be shared as a short link for someone who is not in the room (10-20/21). In the Wallet, community cards are a stack of passes (#519–#546).
- **Directory and polls.** A community's members list and "your card" (#773–#883, with photos). **Community polls** let a member vote as a voter with a **blind ticket** (#884, 11-02), on the screens and versions both sides serve (#892–#912); the vote is carried on behind the ballot (#1155).
- **Sign in to a community's website.** Scan the portal's QR code or open its trigger link, then approve on the phone (`auth/oob`, 10-11; 0.2 with an approval that says how long it lasts, 10-18). The person can sign in as any identity a linked agent holds for that community (10-19). The same flow signs in to the [[vtafarm|VTA Farm]] (above).
- **"Confirm it's you"** (#1032, 11-11; on by default since 11-18, #1148). You can ask a contact to prove with their phone's lock that a call or message in their name is really them; it appears as a card in the chat.
- **did:webs communities.** Keyring can join and sign in to a community whose DID is a `did:webs`. It forwards through the mediator that DID names (bifold #1107–#1116, 11-17), and the resolver is registered in the app (#945). Community sign-in reads the community's `did.json` and does not yet replay its key event log. The app's CESR/KEL verifier, the only KERI verifier in the repository, is kept for that.

**The SEDI demo lane, briefly.** For about a week in mid-November (11-10 → 11-14) Keyring carried a demo of Utah's **State-Endorsed Digital Identity** (SEDI). It landed in the run-up to the Park City SEDI Summit (11-17 → 19), where Keyring's own Brendan Miller (Harvard Applied Technology Lab), the product owner who decides its SEDI plans, joined the Verifiable Trust Infrastructure panel and breakout (see [[overview]]). A state office issued a "State Guardian card" (a KERI/ACDC credential) that the phone verified with its own CESR reader against a State root's key event log fetched from its `did:webs`. A related **trusted approvers** feature ("Who approves": approving for another person's agent, T2/T3, #997–#1023) arrived alongside it. Everything sat behind Developer switches that were off by default, with one kill switch for all of them (#1020). A plan of 11-23 takes the demo, guardianship and trusted approvers off `main` and onto a preserved `sedi-demos` branch, which has been cut. The removal itself is in progress on branches, and the `did:webs` resolution and CESR/KEL verifier that community sign-in uses stay on `main`. The live `sedi-vtc` community, a third party's VTC, is the field test that Keyring can still join it afterwards.

## The feedback loop with upstream

`docs/VTI_UPSTREAM_FINDINGS.md` (v1.44, 2026-09-24) numbers every defect Keyring measured against a VTI stack it can rebuild — forty-three `VTI-NN` findings, each with the command, the observation, the upstream source line and the "era" of the stack it was measured on (eras A through H, plus F and F′ for the Farm). None was filed as an issue; they reach upstream through the document, and upstream answered it as a published remediation plan (their `KR-NN` is Keyring's `VTI-NN`): of thirty-six, twenty-three shipped, four already fixed, three answered in docs, four declined or re-scoped. The cited fixes include the mediator's WebSocket refusal of phones (tdk-rs #831), the per-sender queue cap that let non-collecting members silence a community (#828), consent pushed only to `did:key` approvers (VTI #1579), the `requestMore` request that could never be closed (`withdraw/0.1`, `supplement/0.1`), and **VTI-42**: a VTC's DIDComm router that had never heard of verbs its own service dispatched, resolved by making the binding envelope the only DIDComm carriage on both services — which is why OpenVTC #372 is titled "send every VTC-bound Trust Task in the DIDComm binding envelope (Keyring VTI-42)". Later rounds reached VTI-61 (OpenVTC #415, one relationship store for TSP invites and replies), VTI-13 (join criteria that state their own admission, VTI #1907) and the proof-set round (VTI #1868). `docs/UPSTREAM_ISSUES.md` is the one-page list of what is still open; `docs/VTI_CONFORMANCE_INVENTORY.md` is the table of every document produced or accepted and which upstream code judges it.
