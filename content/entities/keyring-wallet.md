# keyring-wallet — the Keyring phone app

*Repo: [github.com/berkmancenter/keyring-wallet](https://github.com/berkmancenter/keyring-wallet) — the app; its core packages live in the [[keyring-bifold]] submodule. Developed at the Applied Social Media Lab of Harvard's Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society. Apache-2.0. iOS and Android, React Native 0.81.*

Every other client in this wiki assumes a computer: the [[openvtc|OpenVTC TUI]] wants a terminal, the [[vta-browser-plugin|VTA Wallet]] wants Chrome, `pnm` wants a shell. **Keyring is the phone.** It began life outside the OpenVTC ecosystem altogether, as a fork of the OpenWallet Foundation's Bifold wallet (itself the framework under British Columbia's BC Wallet), carrying the Berkman team's own contribution to decentralized trust: peer-to-peer **Verifiable Relationship Credentials** exchanged between two people who met, witnessed by a third party, with the phone's Secure Enclave or StrongBox vouching that a real device and a real person signed. The team drafted the first DTG credential specification with the ToIP working group ([[dtg-credential-spec]]), and the witnessed exchange Keyring ships is the one that spec describes. Since July 2026 the project has turned toward the OpenVTC stack deliberately, as its plan puts it, "contributor, not consumer": it recast its exchange onto Trust Tasks and TSP, linked the phone to a cloud [[verifiable-trust-agent|Verifiable Trust Agent]], and — in a burst of about 1,200 commits across the two repos in September and early October — became the second client after the TUI to carry the **full [[peer-identity-vetting|peer identity vetting]] ceremony, both seats**, on a device a person already owns.

The posture matters as much as the code. Keyring is *not* one of the eighteen repositories in the `VTI-Eucalyptus` tag set; it is an independent downstream that pins upstream by commit, measures it in a lab it can rebuild, and files numbered findings back. Several OpenVTC and VTI fixes in September and October cite "Keyring VTI-NN" in their titles. That feedback loop — a second, independent implementation testing the first — is the thing Trust Task specifications formally need to leave draft status, and Keyring's plan says so explicitly.

## What It Does

**The witnessed relationship exchange — the flagship.** Two phones connect, each issues the other a VRC under a pairwise relationship DID (the Relationship Credential Exchange protocol, RCE v3; `did:peer` R-DIDs, R-Card profile data with an optional photo), and an optional **witness server** attests that the exchange happened in one session by issuing a Verifiable Witness Credential to each side without seeing their private claims ([[witnessed-vrc-exchange]], [[witness-credential]]). "Secure Exchange" adds **hardware attestation**: a P-256 key in the iOS Secure Enclave (App Attest) or Android StrongBox/TEE signs the credential after Face ID, fingerprint or device passcode, and the attestation chain is embedded as W3C evidence and verified natively on the receiving phone — one OS prompt per exchange since 0.2.0. **Locality** adds co-presence: the phone advertises over BLE as a peripheral, the witness's sensor writes a nonce and reads back a transcript signed by the same attestation key, and the proof rides inside the VWC (Android proven live on devices, iOS type-checked only, both shipped behind the witness's opt-in). Credentials are VC 2.0 with `eddsa-rdfc-2022` Data Integrity proofs; everything else a Bifold wallet does — AnonCreds, W3C VCs, Indy VDR, OpenID4VC — is still there through Credo-TS.

**Three transports side by side.** The legacy exchange rides DIDComm v1 over an Aries-style mediator; since 0.2.0 the same documents can ride **DIDComm v2** (Credo's unreleased v2 from PR #2704, consumed as a pinned `0.7.1-pr-2704` snapshot) and **TSP** ([[trust-spanning-protocol]], Rev 3 since 2026-09-21) as a third carriage, with HPKE-Auth on `@noble` so no WebCrypto is needed on Hermes. The team's noble HPKE backend was contributed upstream and became the TSP wire layer of `@openvtc/vti-tsp-js` 0.2.0 at Cypress. The phone↔VTA leg and the phone↔community leg are both Trust Task documents in the published `tsp/0.1` or DIDComm binding envelopes, signed with `eddsa-jcs-2022` document proofs. Keyring does *not* depend on `@openvtc/pnm-core` — it treats that package as a reference and implements the VTA client in its own `@bifold/trust-tasks` and `@bifold/core` (see [[keyring-bifold]]) — but it does take the TSP wire layer from `@openvtc/vti-tsp-js`, vendored as a prebuilt 0.3.0 (Rev 3) tarball from the browser plugin's monorepo with a one-file Hermes patch, because 0.3.x is not yet on npm.

## Keyring as a PNM: the phone over a cloud agent

In the vocabulary of [[vta-topology]], Keyring is a **Personal Network Manager**: a thin surface over an always-on cloud VTA — on the [[vtafarm|VTA Farm]] or self-hosted — that holds the person's keys and answers while the phone sleeps. The phone's own identity is a software key in the wallet; everything community-facing is a **persona** the agent mints and whose keys the phone borrows into memory only while it uses them. The screens never say "VTA" or show a DID: the agent is "your agent", identities are "the code the community sees for you", and every DID sits behind "Show the code".

- **Link.** Scan the agent's enrollment QR or paste its address; the phone signs in with a temporary key, is granted a seat on the agent's access list, and swaps onto a long-lived key — a reducer-driven state machine (`notLinked → confirming → submitting → awaitingGrant → linking → linked`, with `offline`, `reconnecting`, `revoked` and `gone` as sub-states) so leaving and returning to the screen shows the same step. Since 10-01 the Farm's **automatic connection** QR (`{vta_did, callback_url}`) collapses the whole thing into one scan; the callback is treated as a credential and only `ic3.dev` / `firstperson.dev` hosts are allowed.
- **Own.** "Create my agent" (09-25 → 10-01) lets a person with no computer create an agent on a hosting service's website and claim it from the phone: the phone shows the owner code for the Farm's "Admin DID" box, then connects. Owner acts — adding a backup device, removing one — always ask for Face ID again.
- **Devices.** A new phone is a new device (`device/register` 0.2): a devices list with names and last-seen, a one-time offer after a new phone links (keep or remove the old ones), **two phones finishing a link between themselves** (09-28), heartbeats, and a wipe that the agent refuses at login from then on.
- **Several agents.** A row of agent chips across the top of *Your agent*, each with its own count of waiting requests, "Add" at the end; a Wallet card says which agent holds it, cards of an unlinked agent are hidden, and the app looks at the other agents' inboxes when it comes to the foreground.
- **Approvals and consent.** The phone is the agent's approver: a pushed consent request shows the **match code** and what approving would do, and is answered with a signed decision; the agent's `requireStepUp` is answered from the phone with a DID-signed `auth/step-up/approve-response`; **"Ask me before…"** (10-04) sets consent rules from the phone for tasks the phone itself never sends (contexts, keys, ACL changes), lists rules set elsewhere read-only, and warns when one would trap the phone behind its own approval.
- **Push.** Signal-style contentless wake pushes from OpenVTC's push gateway, one wake channel per agent, no Firebase initialized before the person opts in, a notification the phone composes itself (generic text while locked). The gateway image is deployed by this repo's own workflow for its test stack.

## Joining a community from the phone

"I want to join a community" starts from a scanned or pasted `keyring://vti/community` link (or a vetter's ticket, or an invitation), reads the community's **join manifest** (0.2 and 0.3 — a 0.3 criterion states its own admission, `automatic` or `review`, and the screen says a way in admits only when it is automatic), makes the person's identity for that community with one Face ID, and then offers every way in: an invitation from the admin, vetting by a member, a credential the community asks for, or agreement alone. "I was invited" hands the admin the identity to invite (an invitation names one DID, and there is no invitation by reference yet) and redeems the console's QR or DIDComm offer; the join itself presents the [[invitation-credential|invitation]], the vetting statements or both, and on `allow` keeps the [[membership-credential|membership card]], role cards and any vetter grant as Wallet cards. Membership is read back the same way the community means it: removal notices, status lists, `requestAlreadyOpen`, withdraw and supplement, leaving. A community on another mediator is asked over DIDComm from the first message (a TSP Rev 3 relationship did not cross two mediators until the fix in VTI 0.41 / messaging-sdk 0.26.27 — Keyring's VTI-41 — and the Farm's mediator must still be told to admit the relayed hop).

## The vetting flow, on a phone

This is the part the TUI and Keyring now share end to end, and the reason the two were demonstrated side by side for the kernel community in Prague. The ceremony is the one [[peer-identity-vetting]] describes: a **ticket** gates the vetter, a **Vetting Card** carries the applicant's face and legal name, a spoken **match code** binds the person in the room to the client that controls the join DID, and a **Vetting Statement** is what the community counts.

One screen, *Vetting*, seats the person according to what their persona holds: a live `CommunityRole: vetter` grant puts them at the **vetting desk** (its own title, a differently colored header); otherwise it is **their application**. Each step has one filled button, and each step is one screen.

**The applicant.** *Start my application* reads the requirements ("This community needs 2 vetters to confirm your name — 1 in person"), then asks for a face photo and the legal name as on the ID ("the name on your profile may be different" — the card comes from a profile face, as in the TUI). *Ask a vetter*: scan or paste the vetter's ticket; a ticket for another community is refused with a warning that using it would show your identity there. The request goes to the vetter as a signed `vetting/request/0.1` carrying the ticket; the acceptance comes back with the vetter's **eligibility presentation**, which the phone verifies against the manifest's `eligibleVetters.role` before telling the person the statement will count. When the vetter opens the session both phones show the match code ("Read this code aloud — both screens must show the same one"); the applicant confirms it matches, the Vetting Card is sent inside the session's `#response`, and the statement arrives as a signed Trust Task document, checked against the card and the session digest before it is kept. A checklist counts what still is needed by method, discounts statements whose vetter's grant did not cover the moment of signing (checked against the community's status list), caps statements from declared relationships at what the community counts, and lights *Apply* when the criteria are met.

**The vetter's desk.** *New ticket* cuts an eight-character Crockford code with a QR and a link ("Read this code to the person, or let them scan or paste this link"), good for a stated number of uses and a time. Requests arrive under *Requests*; *Open session* sends `vetting/session/0.1` and shows the match code; *Codes match* waits for the card; *Their card* shows the face, the name and the identity code the community sees — the same code the applicant's phone shows under "Your identity in this community", so the two can be compared; **"I checked them and their ID — attest"** issues the statement, and *Statement issued* shows the confirmed name. A vetter can publish a **vetter profile** so applicants can find them in the community's directory, and a desk whose grant was revoked still opens, with the reason it will not sign. Tickets are client-local in V0, exactly as the reference client keeps them.

**What the statement is.** Until 09-30 a Keyring vetter wrote the WD02 `IdentityVetting` endorsement; the community's own in-person identity check is kept as evidence rather than as a vetter's statement. Since the DTG Credentials v1 cutover upstream (VTI 0.47 and OpenVTC `e165bd55`, which refuse the old shape with no dual acceptance), Keyring **reads both shapes always and writes the one the community counts**: the `vetted/1` `StatementCredential` ([[statement-credential]]) with `@context` exactly [VCDM 2.0, registry v1], `taskContext` and `taskDigestMultibase` binding it to the session document, and only the registry's `vetted/1` members in the subject. Since 10-01 it writes v1 by default where a community asks for it; a developer flag forces either.

**Judged by upstream, not by itself.** Three times Keyring's producer and Keyring's consumer agreed with each other and not with upstream (the card digest, the statement, the eligibility VP). The rule since 09-25: every document Keyring signs or checks is judged in CI by upstream's own Rust (`card-verify`, built from `vta-sdk` at the VTI pin, plus a second clone at `vta-service-v0.47.0` so VTI's SDK judges the v1 statement), and an **interop harness** drives Keyring and the real OpenVTC TUI in lockstep in both directions — Keyring vetter / TUI applicant and the reverse — reading assertions from the TUI's state and the community's records. The pinned TUI is `v0.4.0`; the gate's test community lives on the Farm.

**How it compares with the TUI.** Same wire, same ceremony, same V0 limits (client-local tickets, no zero-knowledge k-of-n, no automated document check). What differs is the medium: the phone's camera scans the ticket and the agent's QR, Face ID stands in for the TUI's key handling, the two seats are one screen rather than two pages, identities are never shown as DIDs, and the applicant's identity is minted *before* the ticket because the desk checks the ticket against it — "the same order as the OpenVTC TUI: the community, its requirements, the identity it will know you as, then the application," as the join screen's own comment says.

## The feedback loop with upstream

`docs/VTI_UPSTREAM_FINDINGS.md` (v1.44, 2026-09-24) numbers every defect Keyring measured against a VTI stack it can rebuild — forty-three `VTI-NN` findings, each with the command, the observation, the upstream source line and the "era" of the stack it was measured on (eras A through H, plus F and F′ for the Farm). None was filed as an issue; they reach upstream through the document, and upstream answered it as a published remediation plan (their `KR-NN` is Keyring's `VTI-NN`): of thirty-six, twenty-three shipped, four already fixed, three answered in docs, four declined or re-scoped. The cited fixes include the mediator's WebSocket refusal of phones (tdk-rs #831), the per-sender queue cap that let non-collecting members silence a community (#828), consent pushed only to `did:key` approvers (VTI #1579), the `requestMore` request that could never be closed (`withdraw/0.1`, `supplement/0.1`), and **VTI-42**: a VTC's DIDComm router that had never heard of verbs its own service dispatched, resolved by making the binding envelope the only DIDComm carriage on both services — which is why OpenVTC #372 is titled "send every VTC-bound Trust Task in the DIDComm binding envelope (Keyring VTI-42)". Later rounds reached VTI-61 (OpenVTC #415, one relationship store for TSP invites and replies), VTI-13 (join criteria that state their own admission, VTI #1907) and the proof-set round (VTI #1868). `docs/UPSTREAM_ISSUES.md` is the one-page list of what is still open; `docs/VTI_CONFORMANCE_INVENTORY.md` is the table of every document produced or accepted and which upstream code judges it.

### Implemented vs planned

**Shipped (0.2.0 line, TestFlight and Play internal):** VRC and witnessed exchange as Trust Tasks over DIDComm v1, v2 and TSP; hardware attestation and one-prompt signing; BLE locality (Android proven on devices); R-Card photos and editable multi-profile; agent link, own-agent claim, Farm automatic connection, devices, two-phone linking, several agents, approvals, step-up, "Ask me before…", push wake; join by invitation, by vetting or open, manifest 0.3; the full vetting ceremony both seats with vetted/1 statements; DTG v1 reading throughout; `credential-exchange/query` answered from the wallet; the interop harness Phase 0 and both-direction TUI runs.

**Planned or partial:** the **VSC migration** of the *witness* credential to the `dtg:witnessed` predicate profile (proposal; the predicate vocabulary does not yet exist upstream); **hidden vetting** and the PCS challenge (`vtc/vetting/hidden/*`, `pcs-challenge/0.1`, `vetters/event-mode/0.1` — pinned from trust-tasks 0.27.7 as fixtures only, no client code); the **message-protection** client contract for the mediator's acceptance / refusal / expiry rules; VTA-to-VTA carriage of the relationship exchange (a counterparty's agent answering while their phone sleeps); tickets held on the VTA (`vetting/tickets/*`, V1); iOS locality verified on a device; the interop harness as a nightly Farm job; store release (the app is alpha, not for production).
