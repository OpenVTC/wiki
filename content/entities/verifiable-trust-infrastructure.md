# Verifiable Trust Infrastructure (VTI)

*Repo: [github.com/OpenVTC/verifiable-trust-infrastructure](https://github.com/OpenVTC/verifiable-trust-infrastructure)*

The Verifiable Trust Infrastructure is a Rust workspace containing the core services that power [[verifiable-trust-community|Verifiable Trust Communities]]. It's the infrastructure layer of the OpenVTC ecosystem — the plumbing that makes decentralized trust work.

## Components

The workspace stands at **51 members** at the `VTI-Fig` tag (2026-11-27), up from 32 at `VTI-Eucalyptus`. The history in three steps: in late July 2026 the monolithic `vta-service` was decomposed into eleven subsystem crates (`docs/05-design-notes/vta-service-decomposition.md`); September added a twelfth holder store (`vta-persona`), the four data-rooms crates and, in the Eucalyptus window, `vti-vetting-pcs`; and the Fig window added **nineteen** more. Most of the nineteen are not new features but things pulled *out* so they can be shared and tested on their own — the receive side of a Trust Task service (`vti-trust-task-server`), the approving side of wallet sign-in (`vti-oob-server`), the rooms handlers every host serves (`vti-rooms-host`), the VTC's audit vocabulary (`vtc-audit`), the signing core (`vta-signing`), the mock VTA used in tests (`vta-test-support`) and six **generated wire-type crates** (`vti-specs`, `-core`, `-rooms`, `-vta`, `-vtc`, `-vtn`) that replace hand-written copies of every Trust Task payload. The rest are new capabilities: **[[verifiable-trust-network|Verifiable Trust Network]]** membership (`vti-vtn`), community **voting** on the Predicate Credential System (`vti-voting` and its ballot-box service), the **witness host** (`vti-witness`), **external accounts** (`vta-external`), and an **MCP bridge for a community** (`vtc-mcp`, sharing its guard with `vta-mcp` through `vti-mcp-common`).

Versions are shown at the coordinated **`Cypress`** release ([[coordinated-releases]], 2026-08-17), at **`VTI-Dogwood`** (2026-08-30), at **`VTI-Eucalyptus-RC-0`** (2026-09-17), at the 2026-09-18 interim `chore: release` (#1552), at **`VTI-Eucalyptus`** (2026-10-07, #1985) and at **`VTI-Fig`** (2026-11-27, #2845). ★ = new between Cypress and Eucalyptus; ◆ = new in the Fig window. The `publish = false` members keep a frozen manifest version even though their code moved — read those numbers as labels, not as release markers. At Fig there are eleven of them: vta-enclave, vta-mobile-core, vta-mcp, vtc-mcp, vti-mcp-common, room-host, vti-rooms-wasm, vti-voting-service, vti-witness, didcomm-test and tests/e2e. Two crates went the other way: **`vtc-service` publishes again** (#2192, 2026-10-18 — an operator can `cargo install vtc-service` as they install `pnm` and `cnm`; its frozen 0.11.58 label had been the last copy on crates.io since #938), and `vti-vetting-pcs` publishes because both services depend on it. The size of the jumps (vta-sdk 0.64 → 0.94, vti-common 0.40 → 0.70 in seven weeks) is still release-engineering rather than drama: 155 of the window's 674 commits were marked breaking, and since #1807 moving a sibling's compatibility range is itself a breaking bump. The GA tag is `VTI-Fig-RC-0` plus one commit: kube 5.2.0 for RUSTSEC-2026-0600, which patch-bumped 31 published crates (x.y.0 → x.y.1); the Fig column shows the GA numbers.

The `vti-didcomm-js` crate (JavaScript DIDComm primitives + spec test vectors) was extracted into its own repository in May 2026 — see [[vti-didcomm-js]]. **Publishing** moved in August 2026 to **release-plz** (#938): merging a PR is no longer releasing — release-plz keeps one `chore: release` PR open, and merging *that* tags each crate (`<crate>-v<ver>`), generates per-crate CHANGELOGs from conventional commits, and publishes via crates.io trusted publishing (GitHub OIDC). **40 of 51 members publish** at Fig — and `RELEASING.md` now says so itself, closing the "20 of 26" loose end of the last two cycles. Since September the semver report is a *gate*: a Release PR whose bump is smaller than its API change is blocked (#1256); since #1807 moving a sibling's compatibility range is a breaking bump; and since October the Release PR only releases, its regeneration running one semver check at a time (#2285). Thirty-seven `chore: release` merges landed in the Fig window. On top of that crate-by-crate flow, Fig is the first release cut through **named release branches** (#1989, 2026-10-10; policy and schedule in `RELEASES.md`): `nightly` fast-forwards daily to the newest `main` commit on which *every* CI job passed; a release is cut from `nightly` onto `release/<name>` every four to six weeks; the newest two GA releases are supported (security and deployment-breaking fixes are backported, features never); a release branch publishes **patches only**, in compatibility lines it owns (`releases/<name>.toml`, enforced by `check-release-line-ownership.py`), so `main` can never publish into them; and the project publishes no container or enclave images — operators build from the tag and pin their own PCR0. Eucalyptus was given its branch after the fact (`--released-as`, #1994); Fig was cut from `nightly` on 2026-11-24 (`releases/fig.toml`), tagged `VTI-Fig-RC-0` the next day, and went GA as `VTI-Fig` on 2026-11-27 with a GitHub Release listing all forty published crates. (`RELEASES.md` at the tag still shows Fig's row as "TBD / in development on `main`" — the schedule table was not updated in the cut.) The workspace states that it **implements the ToIP [VTI specification](https://trustoverip.github.io/dtgwg-vti-spec/)** and that the specification is normative where the two disagree (#1403); commits cite its requirement ids (`VTI-KEY-003`, `VTI-ACL-110`, `VTI-NTF-054`, …) in their titles, and the Fig window added whole chapters' worth of them (notifications, boards, badges, voting, VTN), several merged upstream to match what was built.

## How It Fits in the Stack

VTI sits in the middle of the ecosystem stack:

```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│  OpenVTC CLI / TUI          │  ← User-facing tools
├─────────────────────────────┤
│  VTI (VTA + VTC services)   │  ← This layer: key management, signing, coordination
├─────────────────────────────┤
│  Affinidi TDK + WebVH       │  ← DID resolution, messaging, DID hosting
├─────────────────────────────┤
│  didwebvh-rs                │  ← DID method implementation
└─────────────────────────────┘
```

Applications at the top (like [[openvtc|OpenVTC]]) use VTI to manage keys and sign things. VTI in turn uses the [[affinidi-tdk|Affinidi TDK]] for DID resolution and messaging (the mediator it routes through has lived in its own repository since October 2026), and the [[affinidi-webvh-service]] for DID hosting. Since Fig there is also a layer *above* the community: a [[verifiable-trust-network|Verifiable Trust Network]] service, in its own repository, built on the `vti-vtn` crate this workspace shares with it.

## Tech Stack

- **Language**: Rust (edition 2024, `rust-version` 1.95); since #2709 the compiler is **pinned** — `rust-toolchain.toml` at 1.100.0 (#2714), with CI and both Dockerfiles held to it — so a new stable release's lints can no longer turn `main` red with no code change
- **Async runtime**: Tokio
- **HTTP**: Axum 0.8; `/livez` and `/readyz` probes beside `/health` on both services (#2777)
- **Storage**: fjall (embedded LSM), with indexed reads for the VTC's hot full-keyspace scans and the rooms' records since #2711/#2712
- **Identity**: did:webvh and **did:scid:vh:1** as durable identities, and — alpha — **did:webs** on KERI v2, which a VTA, a VTC or a member may now hold (see Recent Development)
- **Crypto**: ed25519-dalek 3, x25519-dalek, p256, k256; ML-DSA-44 / ML-DSA-65 post-quantum signing; MLS (openmls) for data rooms; BLS12-381 pairing-based zero-knowledge proofs (arkworks, via `predicate-credential-system`) for hidden-vetter admission and unlinkable voting tickets

- **Supply chain**: Dependabot-managed bumps, every GitHub Action pinned to a commit SHA, every Docker base pinned to a digest (the Nitro image's apk set too, so PCR0 moves only with a commit), release tokens validated first and taken from a GitHub App when one is configured, `cargo deny` advisories scanned daily on `main` and every release branch, fuzz targets run nightly (#2709); a `SECURITY.md` with private reporting and the supported releases (#2812)

## Recent Development

This page is the canonical activity log for the VTI workspace. The [[verifiable-trust-agent|VTA entity]] keeps a focused, VTA-relevant subset. Implementation continues to evolve quickly; treat low-level details as in flux.

The Fig window (2026-10-07 → 11-27: 674 commits, PRs #1987 → #2845; 616 by one maintainer, 42 by Dependabot, 16 by eight others; 37 `chore: release` merges; 155 commits marked breaking and 11 `security(...)` commits) is the largest VTI has had by commit count, and it ends at **`VTI-Fig`** — the first release cut on its own branch, and one of twenty repositories tagged together ([[coordinated-releases]]). ### did:webs and did:scid — who a node is — 2026-10-23 → 11-18

Until Fig a durable VTI identity was a did:webvh, full stop. Two other methods arrived, for different reasons. **`did:scid:vh:1`** (#2257, 10-23) is a did:webvh log presented under its self-certifying identifier, so the DID does not name the host that serves it: a VTA can mint new DIDs as did:scid by default (`[dids] default_did_method`), every identity comparison goes by DID and ignores the `?src=` locator, and `vta/webvh/dids/update/1.1` lists or removes a log's did:scid alias (#2354). **`did:webs`** is a DID backed by a KERI key event log with pre-rotated keys, which matters for identities whose holder must be able to recover from a key compromise, and for interoperating with KERI ecosystems such as the ones behind state-issued credentials. The work came in layers. First the resolver learned to verify one (#2505, 11-09), a member could rotate onto one (#2517), and a node would act toward a did:webs peer only if it declared did:webs itself (#2673). Then the VTA learned to create, host, update, rotate and delete did:webs DIDs and act as one as a persona (#2561–#2564, `pnm webs`). Then a VTA's *own* identity could be a **key-role did:webs**, with separate attestation, operational and messaging keys, set up at first boot by self-registration (#2675, #2679) — including inside a TEE (#2696) — provisioning integrations and rotating its role keys from its key event log (#2685), and a VTC could take a level-1 did:webs as its DID (#2698). Finally **every DID the VTA holds goes through one method-neutral `vta/dids/*` surface** (#2763, 11-18), with one engine per kind of authority (the webvh log, KERI, did:key), so a context delete now ends a did:webs on its host instead of leaving its key event log published as a live identity. VTI creates KERI v2 identifiers only (`docs/05-design-notes/did-webs-keri-v2.md`, decided 11-10) and labels the whole integration **alpha**: a did:webs created now may have to be created again as the KERI and did:webs specifications settle. The key-roles design behind it (`key-roles.md`: key assurance levels 1–3, a recovery key required only at level 3, threshold recovery custody) was revised on 10-30 and resumed on 11-09; its fifteen-step implementation plan was still at step 0 at the tag, waiting on the specification PRs.

### Communities that federate, govern and talk — October–November 2026

Most of the window's 108 `feat(vtc)` commits are community features, and each has its own page; here is where they sit in the code.

### Hardening — step-up verifies the user, answers that leak nothing — October–November 2026

- **Step-up.** `operations::step_up_approval` signed a VTA approval after a user-verification gate that always returned true (#2702). Its only caller had been deleted in #1739, so nothing reached it, but any future caller could have handed out a VTA-signed approval with no human behind it; #2793 removed it. The largest stream, and the one the release is named for. ### A document's proof is the authority — sender binding, proof purpose, signed replies — 2026-09-22 → 09-27

Closing the door was only useful if what comes through it is checked. The VTC's dispatcher had verified a proof *when one was present* and otherwise trusted the transport; **#1659 (breaking) enforces what each specification declares** — `recipient`, `proof`, audience, `issuedAt` within 10 min + 60 s skew, a replay record bounded to that window — on every transport, and **#1672 flips "a declared proof is required" to unconditional** once OpenVTC (openvtc#371) signed its five join documents, deleting the switch and recording that no setting may put it back. Then **#1739 (breaking): every DIDComm and TSP Trust Task needs a Data Integrity proof that verifies as its `issuer`, and that issuer must be the transport's sender** (`proofRequired` / `proofInvalid` / `identityMismatch`); the sender-authorized protocol-message surface it replaces is removed outright. Both services **sign every response, errors included, with their operational key under `proofPurpose: authentication`** (#1740, VTI-KEY-106), and a verifier now checks a proof's key against the relationship the proof claims (#1752, breaking — a key published only for `keyAgreement` could make an `assertionMethod` proof; a proof *set* is accepted only if every proof verifies). An approver's decision needs `assertionMethod` (#1757); the mobile engine signs under `authentication` and **verifies every reply**, including errors (#1744). Lower down: an authcrypt sender is trusted only when `skid`, `apu` and the key actually used agree (#1732); **one bounded DID-document cache per node** with a 60 s TTL and one re-resolution before a verification fails, so a revoked key stops verifying within a minute and a rotated peer is not refused as a forger (#1737, VTI-KEY-134); a resolver failure is told apart from an invalid proof on the two caller-side paths where that is not an oracle (#1748, FTL-29595); a signed REST sign-in must be addressed to the service that receives it, closing a relay (#1646); the challenge is consumed atomically before minting (#1664). Design note: `vtc-trust-task-proof-enforcement.md`.

### Key custody and the scope sweep — 2026-09-21 → 09-26

Reported as FTL-29904: a *context-scoped* admin could list and rotate the instance-wide seed. Reviewing it found four holes with one shape — a gate that asked about the caller's role or context, never about the path or key the caller named — including `keys/create` with a `derivationPath` deriving *any* key (another tenant's, the VTA's own did:webvh update key) and exporting it, and a vault entry naming `{vta_did}#key-0` as its signing key so the VTA signed as itself. **#1715 (breaking) states the custody rules and enforces them in the operations, not the transports** (`vta-keys/src/custody.rs`, `key-custody.md`): seed state is instance-wide; a context's key derives from that context's base and no other; only a super-admin chooses a path; `m/26'/9'` is the sign-only delegated-identity subtree; rules are checked at *use*, so a planted record is inert; every refusal is audited. The sweep that followed scoped sessions and consent grants to the caller's authority (#1717), step-up approval (which had signed "this VTA approved a step-up" for any authcrypt sender), audit verify, policy reads and device management (#1720), applied the `key-export` and `sign` capability checks on every transport — **private keys are no longer released over REST or the HTTPS binding at all** (#1733, #1743, breaking) — made `key-export` its own capability and DID minting need `KeyMint` rather than admin (#1619), gated `vta/contexts/secrets` on `KeyExport` (#1634), and closed the ACL's self-service: **no principal widens its own entry and no grant exceeds its granter** (#1738, VTI-ACL-052/053 — a companion service's credential could clear its own narrowing, and `swap-key` rebuilt an entry field by field so a one-hour bootstrap grant became permanent). Also: **no type derives `Debug` over secret material** — about 55 types across twelve crates, kept closed by a `syn` census (#1711); **refresh-token reuse detection** with a 30-second innocent-retry grace, revoking the session a replayed token belongs to (#1650, RFC 9700), and the superseded refresh token retired when a DID logs in again (#1683); the per-IP limiter keyed on trusted-proxy CIDRs instead of a global XFF flag (#1562, breaking config); a deactivated did:webvh no longer resolves to its last document (#1881 — the one commit on the public `SEC-4045` thread, shared with VGI). Holder authority over the persona pool is **granted, never inherited** — super-admin had carried it anyway (#1673); did:webvh key rotation now rotates each method **in place with its own algorithm** instead of republishing X25519 and ML-DSA methods with Ed25519 keys (#1734, breaking); and **a backup is the whole agent** — format `vta-backup-v2` walks every `BACKED_UP` keyspace (ten had never been exported), stages the import, commits the seed and re-execs, and restores between plain, hardened and Nitro VTAs in all eight pairings (#1655, breaking; `backup-restore-portability.md`).

### DTG Credentials v1 — role VACs, vetted/1, witnessed/1, issuerScope — 2026-09-23 → 10-02

dtg-credentials moved 0.9 → 0.11 (#1669) → 0.13, and **#1859 (breaking) conforms every credential the VTC issues and the SDK reads to the DTG Credentials Core v1 context**: roles are [[authority-credential|Authority Credentials]] whose action is `role:<name>` with `maxAttenuation: 0`; endorsements, witnessing and vetting are [[statement-credential|Statement Credentials]] under a registry predicate (`endorses/1`, `witnessed/1`, `vetted/1`, `presented/1` — the four core predicates seeded at first boot as the community's fail-closed accept list); every DTG credential carries a top-level `issuerScope`; the retired `EndorsementCredential` and `WitnessCredential` types are refused on ingress with no alias; `roleVec` became `roleVac` on the wire. Room credentials declare `issuerScope` under the v1 context too. Because Rego treats a missing field as undefined, **a custom policy written against the old shapes silently denies** — `docs/03-vtc/policy-upgrade-dtg-v1.md` (#1871) walks operators through `foreign_vec → foreign_vac`, the witnessed digest moving to `object.digestMultibase`, and the community's own identity check, which is **no longer a plain `IdentityVerificationCredential` but a `vetted/1` statement issued under the community's own DID** (#1874 — the registry's `vetted/1` admits the community as issuer, with the three vetter-only members optional and all-or-nothing; a statement without them is personhood evidence, never admission evidence). ### Vetting — hidden vetters on a Predicate Credential System, and the first-vetter problem — 2026-09-21 → 10-06

[[peer-identity-vetting]] was designed for a concrete deadline: OpenVTC's `docs/design/vetting-process.md` targeted V0 at 2026-10-05 in Prague, ahead of the Linux Kernel Maintainer Summit (10-08), and the project was presented at the Linux Plumbers Conference on 10-07 ("Cryptographic Proofs of Personhood: Solving the Kernel Web of Trust Problem in a Privacy-Preserving Manner"). The VTI half of the window reflects that. **Named vetting** gained the pieces a real community hits first: a community can ask an applicant to tell it about themselves (`requestedAttributes` on the manifest, self-asserted and never fed to policy; `cnm vetting ask`, #1614, breaking), an applicant can withdraw or supplement a request in place (#1591, #1593), a by-DID vetter status lookup (#1671), the `cnm vetting`/`audit`/`backup` commands authenticate to the VTC with the *VTC's* DID as audience rather than failing against the VTA's (#1637), and **join criteria state their own admission** — `automatic` admits, `review` refers to an administrator (#1907, Keyring VTI-13). The **bootstrap runbook** (`docs/03-vtc/bootstrap-runbook.md`, #1627) records two Keyring findings that needed documentation, not code: a fresh VTC's ACL is empty *including for the admin DID setup prints*, and **an admin is not a member, so the first vetter is a separate identity admitted before any criterion requires vetting**. `cnm vetting bootstrap-pgp`, which seeds a community's first vetters from an existing OpenPGP web of trust (keyring + root keys + clearsigned link statements, everything local verified before the community is contacted), landed at the end of the RC window (#1432) and is what that runbook's Part 2 points a kernel-shaped community at. **Hidden vetting** is new in the window: `vti-vetting-pcs` (#1838) lets an applicant prove, in zero knowledge, that *k* distinct vetters of this community vetted them; the VTC verifies the proof and counts it, learning nothing about which vetters — each vetter's *tag* stands where their DID would, so `requirements::evaluate` and `join.rego` are untouched, a spent-token ledger is a new backed-up keyspace, and the whole path sits behind `vtc-service`'s off-by-default `vetting-pcs` feature on the `zkp-pcs` development branch (the `predicate-credential-system` crate was vendored, then taken from crates.io). The console turns it on, off, reads it back and runs vetting *events* (#1955, #1956 — `vtc/vetting/hidden/{publish,withdraw,show}/0.1`, with approvals recorded in the approving administrator's own name); the attestation **drip is a window of time** (`tickLength`, default `P3D`), a signing-key change refuses to re-key silently, and it runs on one criterion (#1960); a vetter who lost the enrollment answer is re-issued it to the same identifier only, three times per label (#1972); and republishing the drip rate no longer voids every attestation in flight, which had held a live join (#1977). Refusals say why (#1954, #1961, #1976). See [[zero-knowledge-proofs]].

### Git namespaces — forge governance published to the Trust Registry — 2026-09-24 → 10-06

A VTC can now **govern repositories on the forges its community uses — GitHub, a Forgejo instance such as Codeberg — and publish who may do what there to its Trust Registry**, where `did-git-sign verify-trust` reads it (`docs/03-vtc/git-namespaces.md`; the consumer is [[verifiable-git-infrastructure]]). **#1694** landed the `git-ns/*` family: a namespace binds the VTC to one owner on one forge; rights (`git.ns.admin`, `git.repo.create`, `git.repo.own`, `git.repo.maintain`, `git.commit.sign`) are held by DIDs on forge-qualified resources keyed by an internal repository id so a rename moves them; the **fixed rules are code, not policy** (whole-segment containment, no escalation, `repo.create` not re-delegable, a repository keeps an owner, a namespace keeps an admin, members only) and the community's `gitNamespace` Rego policy can only refuse; one TRQP authorization record per live right is written to the registry with the implied `git.commit.sign` of every owner, maintainer and admin — **who granted a right and why is never published**; bridge jobs travel signed over TSP or DIDComm; a namespace admin gets **no role on the forge** (#1729). `cnm git {namespace,repos,grant,revoke,adopt,view,link,unlink,drift,reseat}` and the console's **Repos** plugin (#1696) followed, with drift resolve and headless-namespace reseat (#1703), forge-account linking with one account per member (#1726, #1742, #1746), **separation of duties and a ratified break-glass for elevated rights** (#1745), and `view/0.4` requiring a proof (#1770). Then **#1930 (breaking) folded the rights store into the ACL — one authority model**: every git right is a *resource grant* on its holder's own entry (`git.ns.admin` at `git-ns:github.com/acme`; `git.repo.manage` graded `create`/`own`/`maintain`; `git.commit.sign`), bounded by the granter's own entry, with a departed granter's grants going to review; a non-member holding a grant (the bridge, an external signer) holds it on an entry of the new community role `application`, which never signs in. A **pull-request gate** closes PRs from openers the policy does not allow — "hygiene, not the merge gate" (#1937, `bridge/event/0.4`, `bridge/job/0.5`); single-administrator mode waives the self-grant rule where nobody else is eligible (#1936); a suspended subject's rights are withdrawn from registry and forge for the duration of a cooling-off (#1945); a community serves `governance/capability/*` so OpenVTC's Capabilities panel can list and enable capability modules with the registry following (#1948). On the VTA side, **`keys/sign-sshsig/0.1` signs a git commit without exporting the key** (#1957): `did-git-sign` had been calling `keys/export-secret` on every commit because the opaque oracle frames caller bytes under a domain tag — exactly the hardening that made SSHSIG impossible — so the VTA now builds the `PROTOCOL.sshsig` signed data itself under a constrained `sign-sshsig` capability.

### Role-based administration, consent and the action list — 2026-09-25 → 10-05

At RC-0 a VTC administrator was "an `admin` entry with an empty context list", one stolen admin credential with its passkey could mint a second unrestricted admin, and a context-scoped admin could invite a DID it controlled into community-wide authority. The window rebuilt this in three phases, each with a design note first (#1914: `vtc-admin-roles.md`, `vtc-action-list.md`, `vtc-approver-step-up.md`; operator guide `docs/03-vtc/admin-access.md` with an infographic, #1915, #1933). **Consent.** The task-consent core moved into vti-common so the VTC runs the VTA's own ceremony (#1730): **making or widening an unrestricted administrator needs another unrestricted administrator's consent** (#1741, VTI-APV-014), invites and attrition respect it so the last administrator able to consent cannot step down behind a scoped one (#1747), a co-admin can be installed at setup and the offline ACL break-glass is audited (#1750), and approvers answer from `cnm consent` or `pnm consent` as well as a device (#1759, #1761). **The action list** (#1918, breaking, VTI-APV-017): a consent-gated operation is no longer refused and re-sent — it is *parked* in a new `admin_actions` keyspace with the requester's signed document verbatim, answered `trust-task-next-step` (202), and **completes by itself on the N-th approval**, re-running every check against the community as it then is; one deny closes it, it expires, and it is invalidated when the pinned state moves. Removing an administrator, lowering the consent threshold and changing an authority-deciding policy take a second party (#1917); operator writes made offline must be acknowledged at the next boot, a reduction sends its subject a signed notice and an unopposed reduction waits out a **24-hour cooling-off** during which the subject is suspended (#1920, #1944, #1922); an **approver device can be an administrator's step-up factor** — the browser plugin's approver identity answers for a wallet-signed-in admin who holds no passkey (#1919, VTI-APV-015/016); once a dedicated step-up passkey exists it is the only gesture that counts (#1850; the VTA matches it, #1854); members get self-revoke and takeover notices for their step-up passkeys (#1872, #1875). **Roles.** **#1924 (breaking): administration is role-based** — a typed registry of twenty capabilities with authority-conferring flags, seven built-in roles (`community-admin`, `moderator`, `vetting-lead`, `repo-manager`, `credential-officer`, `auditor`, and the least-privilege `approver`), explicit act scope, resource qualifiers (a git namespace, a policy purpose, a join criterion), `acl/*/0.2` beside 0.1, and an audited in-place migration of every pre-role ACL row at boot; **custom roles**, approver sets read off approve authority alone, a departed granter's grants reviewed as an action, and **`acl/swap-key`** rolling an entry to a new key with exactly its authority (#1927; `cnm` now holds one identity per community and rotates it this way, #1923); **single-administrator mode**, set at install, for the community with nobody to consent — the requester's bound step-up stands in, every waiver audited Critical with a permanent console banner (#1925, #1941). The console became **live**: one hint-only event stream per session over the ordinary `/v1/trust-tasks` door as an HTTPS-binding 0.3 streamed response — never a record, an id or a DID on the wire (#1932); break-glass ratification, join review and vetting review wait in the same Actions list (#1931). An administrator may *withdraw* a member's registry consent, never grant it (#1962), and the registry syncer publishes only members who consented (#1691).

### Trust Tasks over TSP Rev 3 — relationships, one outbound path, multi-hop privacy — 2026-09-15 → 09-18

The largest post-Dogwood stream, and the one that reverses a design doubt. `tsp-vs-didcomm-trust-task-surface.md` was written to ask whether the §7.2.2 relationship layer was worth its cost; the answer, recorded as **decided**, is that multi-hop TSP — routing where intermediaries never learn the final recipient — is a *requirement*, so the recovery work is "justified investment, not speculative rent". What landed: **Rev 3 adopted** (#1512, breaking — a flag day, since nothing a Rev 2 peer packs can be unpacked by a Rev 3 one; inventory in `tsp-rev3-migration.md`); Trust Tasks carried in the **TSP binding envelope** (#1478, #1488); **one outbound path** chosen from what a peer advertises, replacing four hand-rolled carriages (room-host REST, webvh DIDComm, eight webvh verbs, device push — #1474), with the webvh selector folded onto it so a TSP did-host is reached over TSP (#1483) and a did-host advertising where it accepts Trust Tasks (#1476); replies correlated in the spine so TSP can *initiate* (#1482); TSP relationships **persisted across restarts** (#1531), formed before a send or a health ping (#1527, #1537, #1540), answered instead of ignored (#1525), re-formed on a reply timeout on the client (D4, #1544), the VTC registry client (#1546) and the VTA's server-initiated sends via a single-flight `RecoveryCoordinator` (D6, #1549), with boot enumeration + idle eviction (#1534) and relationship-gate drops surfaced as telemetry (D8, #1536); and — after the RC — **cross-mediator sends nested for metadata privacy** (#1559): our mediator never learns the recipient. One TSP transport, and it must carry a mediator (#1507). The `tsp-relationship-recovery.md` note tracks D1–D9; D7/D9 hardening remain open. #1560 (breaking) then fixed the transport seam to select by the sender's *live* capability rather than the build feature — the post-Dogwood VTC-setup regression.

### Post-quantum: ML-DSA keys, hybrid credentials, did-templates 3.0 — 2026-09-16 → 09-18

`KeyType` gains **`MlDsa44` and `MlDsa65`** (#1502, breaking via `#[non_exhaustive]`) — two parameter sets because two specs require different ones: W3C Quantum-Resistant Cryptosuites define Data Integrity suites for ML-DSA-44, TSP Rev 3 §8.1 mandates ML-DSA-65. Keys derive from the BIP-32 chain (#1505); a derived key **carries the algorithm it was minted with** rather than a literal at every save site (#1532, breaking); an operator can create one and see which axis it protects (#1535). did-templates gain a `keys` block declaring which algorithms a template's slots use (#1530, breaking), a third slot beyond the classical pair (#1554 — the previous rejection's own advice would have published a `PLACEHOLDER-NEVER-SUBSTITUTED` literal into a write-once did:webvh log), and the VTA accepts **did-templates 3.0 alongside 2.0** (#1538) with clients carrying a post-quantum template (#1542). On the VTC: verification reads a document carrying *more than one proof* (#1548, #1550), and the RC commit itself (#1553) has **the VTC sign with every key it holds** — one proof per key, each under its own cryptosuite, byte-identical output for a single-key VTC so nothing downstream changes until a second key exists. Its most consequential finding: `affinidi-data-integrity` was pinned without its `ml-dsa` feature, so every capability built above it led to a key that could not sign — "built, tested, and unreachable". Post-RC: a sealed-transfer template-bootstrap variant carrying a second signing key (#1556) and a VTC provisioned from the v2 template issuing hybrid credentials (#1557).

### Security sprint — audit chain, domain-separated oracle, exportability, endpoint vetting, CI supply chain — 2026-09-10 → 09-12

Nine `security(...)` commits and the audit work, most closing requirements of the VTI specification's divergence register. **The VTA's audit log is now a hash chain** (`vta-audit-chaining.md`, #1408; #1412 → #1421): its own audit key from the OS random source, deliberately *not* seed-derived so a mnemonic holder cannot recompute it (#1419); each entry commits to its predecessor, with actors and DID-shaped targets under a keyed hash beside the plaintext so an erasure can null the plaintext while the chain still verifies (#1420); a `verify` pass that separates the counts a bare pass/fail hides (#1421); pre-chain keyspaces served (#1413); and failed audit writes *reported* rather than `let _ =`-swallowed at 27 call sites, plus the signing oracle finally leaving a `keys.sign` row (#1443, breaking — `sign_payload` takes a required audit sink). The **opaque signing oracle is domain-separated** (#1417, breaking): unparseable payloads are framed under a domain tag so a signature obtained for one purpose cannot verify as an assertion, token or proof in another protocol. A key can be marked **never leaving the VTA** (`exportable`, #1401, #1407), and `keys/export-secret` replaces the misnamed, unspecced, global-Admin-gated `seeds/export-mnemonic` (#1404). The SDK **vets DID-advertised VTA endpoints** (private, loopback, link-local, CGNAT … refused) and binds cached tokens to their origin (#1436, breaking); the resolver refuses did:webvh resolution to non-public hosts by default (#1448, breaking); cnm checks who sealed an admin credential before installing it (#1437); backup exports are written 0600 with `create_new` (#1438); the enclave proxy resolves `nitro-cli` by absolute path with a scrubbed environment (#1441); `/auth/challenge` no longer discloses enrolment (#1405); a plugin icon renders as an image, not markup (#1442). CI: **every action pinned to a full commit SHA** — 58 refs, none pinned, and the log showed a `@stable` ref drifting within nine days on a job holding `id-token: write` (#1440); workflow inputs validated through env (#1434); rustls 0.23.45 for RUSTSEC-2026-0285 (#1468).

A 2026-06-22 decision record (`docs/05-design-notes/messaging-routing-and-tsp.md`) initially **deferred** TSP because the Affinidi mediator lacked TSP routing and `affinidi-tsp` was inert scaffolding — then both blockers cleared upstream within three days, and an SDD (`docs/05-design-notes/tsp-enablement.md`, #579) reversed the decision and flipped transport preference guidance to **TSP > DIDComm > REST**, effective immediately. Key locked decisions: DIDs are TSP VIDs reusing existing Ed25519/X25519 keys (no new key material); one dual-protocol mediator serves both TSP and DIDComm; capability discovery is DID-document-driven, matched by service `type` not `#id` fragment (ids renamed to `#didcomm` / `#tsp` / `#rest`); a `tsp` cargo feature mirrors `didcomm`, off by default initially.

Implementation landed as a stacked PR train (2026-06-25 → 07-03): `tsp` feature across sdk/service/enclave/vtc (#580); `TSPTransport` service patchers with TSP-first canonical ordering (#581) and advertisement in DID templates (#584); a peer-matching engine for capability discovery + protocol selection (#583); TSP as a first-class managed service — `ServiceState::Tsp` with enable/update/disable/rollback over REST, DIDComm, and CLI (#585–#589) and declarative setup (#598); a TSP inbound listener over the shared mediator websocket (#595, #601 — no second socket, no mediator flapping); vault unsealing of `tsp-message` sealed envelopes (#594); a TSP round-trip health probe (`pnm health` TSP ping, #610–#618); and an operator guide (`docs/02-vta/tsp.md`). Strictly additive at the time — feature-gated and opt-in; by August TSP had become a selectable transport with TSP-only VTAs supported (see above).

### v0.5.0 — 2026-05-04 — `sealed-bootstrap` major release
