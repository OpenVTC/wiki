# Trust Tasks

A Trust Task is the unit of work in the OpenVTC ecosystem: a single, versioned JSON document that says *who* is asking *whom* to do *what*, signed by the asker, and carried over whichever transport the two parties share. Every exchange in the stack — granting a role, joining a community, issuing a credential, witnessing a relationship, backing up an agent, administering a mediator — is one of these documents, with a canonical `https://trusttasks.org/spec/<family>/<task>/<MAJOR.MINOR>` type URI. The framework is a Working Draft of the Trust Over IP Foundation's Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group, and the Eucalyptus release ([[coordinated-releases]], 2026-10-07) is the point at which the ecosystem finished betting on it: every call into the stack is a signed Trust Task, and the REST management surfaces that once stood beside it are gone.

## Why

The problem Trust Tasks solve is stated plainly in the framework's own introduction: parties cooperate over a network by agreeing on the shape of the work, and today that agreement is reached ad hoc — every onboarding flow and consent receipt described in a vendor schema, carried on a vendor protocol, validated by vendor code — producing "a combinatorial explosion of pairwise integrations." This ecosystem felt the explosion in miniature. It has three carriers for the same operations: [[trust-spanning-protocol|TSP]] (preferred), [[didcomm|DIDComm]] and plain HTTPS. Without a shared document, "change this member's role" would be three message formats with three authentication stories, and a feature added to one carrier would silently drift from the other two. A Trust Task is *the task*; the carrier is a detail chosen per peer from what the peer's DID document advertises.

The second problem is schema drift. Before the Dogwood cycle the VTC's handler responses had diverged from their published schemas in 33 places; a conformance sweep drove that to zero and the [[verifiable-trust-infrastructure|VTI]] began enforcing the framework at its dispatch spine (#1146, 2026-08-27). Generated types in four languages, built from the same `payload.schema.json`, are how the drift stays at zero.

The third is evidence. The VTI specification's rationale puts it in one sentence: transport authentication says *who opened this connection*; a document proof says *who authored this document, what it covers, and to whom it was addressed*, and "only the second survives the message being stored, forwarded, replayed on another transport, or produced in evidence afterwards." A signed Trust Task is a retainable record — "a named party asked a named party to do a named thing at a named instant" — which is what a trust graph needs when a role grant or a membership is later disputed. The VTI's September design notes call the resulting entry point the **signed door**: an operation reachable only as a signed document, with any human step-up bound to that one operation's payload digest rather than to a session (#1641 series, 2026-09-24 → 09-25).

## What a Trust Task Is

**One object, no envelope.** The framework deliberately defines no wrapper. `id` (globally unique, UUIDv4 recommended), `type` (the type URI, whose trailing segment is the specification version), `issuer` and `recipient` (verifiable identifiers — DIDs here), `issuedAt`, optional `expiresAt`, optional `threadId` / `parentThreadId` for correlation, an optional `ceremony` member recording membership of a multi-task flow, the task-specific `payload`, and an optional `proof`. Everything but `payload` is framework-owned, so one framework schema validates the outside and one per-task schema validates the inside.

**Three replies, told apart by type alone.** Success is the same URI with `#response`; failure is `trust-task-error` with a standard code vocabulary (`proofRequired`, `proofInvalid`, `malformedRequest`, `idConflict`, `expired`…), `retryable` and `retryAfter`; a continuation is `trust-task-next-step`. A consumer must never infer the disposition from the payload. Each reply is itself a Trust Task, so one validation, signing and transport pipeline serves both directions.

**Proof and sender binding.** `proof` is a W3C Data Integrity proof with `eddsa-jcs-2022` as the baseline every consumer must verify; its `verificationMethod` must resolve to key material controlled by the `issuer` *and* be authorized for the stated `proofPurpose` (tightened 2026-09-26, #637). In-band identity is authoritative and transport identity is a cross-check: a document whose `issuer` disagrees with the authenticated DIDComm or TSP sender is rejected. A proof without a `recipient` is rejected too — the audience-binding rule — unless the specification is explicitly a bearer one. The framework's §9.1.1 forbids a transport binding from quietly waiving proofs: "silence is not permission." That clause is why the VTI's #1739 (2026-09-26) could require a sender-bound document proof on every DIDComm and TSP task — the DIDComm binding's own text says a REQUIRED proof "is mandatory regardless of transport, because such specifications produce documents intended to be replayable past the original transport hop."

**Replay, freshness, idempotency.** Consumer requirement 11 makes duplicate-execution protection normative for any *consequential* task (one that mutates state, discloses a secret or acts as the subject): once a document `id` is accepted, the same document arriving again must not have its effect twice, and a same-`id`-different-content arrival is an `idConflict`. Item 13 bounds that record with an acceptance window on `issuedAt`, which is why every consequential specification must require `issuedAt`. The Rust runtime ships these as `ReplayGuard` and `FreshnessPolicy`; the bindings absorb a mediator redelivery rather than run the task again (#276); the VTA's `idempotencyKey` dedup sits on top for the lost-reply case. Since discovery 0.3 (2026-09-28) a responder advertises its acceptance window.

**Consent and step-up are Trust Tasks too.** Delegated Trust-Task Execution — the VTA's approval ceremony — is three URIs (`task-consent/request`, `decision`, `granted`) with no REST endpoints of its own; the approver signs a decision echoing a challenge over a digest of the exact task URI and payload. `auth/step-up` and `consent/approve-request` are likewise registered tasks with `proof` REQUIRED. Credentials issued inside an exchange point back at it through [[trust-task-context-binding|taskContext]], and multi-task flows are **Trust Ceremonies** (ADR 0001, 2026-08-09) evidenced by a `trust-ceremony-receipt`.

## The Registry and the Task Force

The task force repository (trustoverip/dtgwg-trust-tasks-tf) is the registry: one folder per task at `specs/<slug>/<MAJOR.MINOR>/` holding a `spec.md` with normative front matter (parties, proof requirement, side-effect and exposure class, error codes, target framework version) and a `payload.schema.json`. A new family arrives as a pull request with DCO sign-off, `CODEOWNERS` routes it to the namespace's editors, and the build validates schemas, example documents and the category taxonomy before regenerating the site at trusttasks.org. The framework text itself has been generated from a canonical sibling repo, dtgwg-trust-tasks-spec, since 2026-09-21.

At Eucalyptus the registry holds 39 families and roughly 690 spec versions: the ecosystem's own surfaces (`vta/*`, `vtc/*`, `did-management/*`, `webvh/*`, `messaging/*`, `registry/*`) and cross-cutting ones (`acl/*`, `auth/*`, `keys/*`, `vault/*`, `credential-exchange/*`, `task-consent/*`, `backup/*`, `push/*`…). The four families named in the Eucalyptus summary were authored in September alongside the features they describe: `rooms/*` (2026-09-02, [[data-rooms]]), `persona/*` (09-05, behind [[persona-credential]]), `vetting/*` (09-11, [[peer-identity-vetting]]) and `git-ns/*` (09-23, [[community-git-namespaces]]). Transport bindings have their own namespace, `trusttasks.org/binding/<name>/<version>`: `https` 0.3, `didcomm` 0.2, `didcomm-v1` 0.2 (for Aries-lineage agents such as [[keyring-bifold]]), `tsp` 0.1 (Rev 3 since 2026-09-16) and `push` 0.1. Framework revisions ran 0.1 (May), 0.2 (June, lowerCamelCase with a dual-accept window), 0.3.0 and 0.4.0 (August 8–9), 0.5.0 (08-26), 0.6.0 (09-21) and 0.7.0 (09-24).

The libraries are generated from the registry and released in lockstep: in Rust `trust-tasks-rs` with `-proof`, `-https`, `-didcomm`, `-didcomm-v1`, `-tsp`, `-capability-client`, `-ceremony` and a `trust-tasks` facade; in TypeScript `@openvtc/trust-tasks` and its proof, TSP and capability-client siblings; and, since 2026-09-16, Go (`trust-tasks-go`) and Dart (`trust_tasks`), each with the §7.2 consumer runtime and proof, HTTPS, DIDComm and TSP bindings.

## Who Speaks It

The [[verifiable-trust-agent|VTA]] and [[verifiable-trust-community|VTC]] serve and consume them over all three carriers; the mediator answers its own management tasks; the DID hosting service and the [[trust-registries|trust registry]] (`registry/*`, TRQP) are reached by Trust Task only. On the client side: the [[openvtc]] TUI; the [[vta-browser-plugin|browser wallet]], which signs every outbound task at the channel and uses [[vti-didcomm-js]] to treat the mediator as a Trust Task counterparty; the [[keyring-wallet|Keyring]] phone, whose `@bifold/trust-tasks` package carries `eddsa-jcs-2022` proofs and the DIDComm v1 `~attach` carriage, and whose [[witnessed-vrc-exchange|witnessed VRC exchange]] is being recast onto `vrc/relationships/*` and `witness/*`; `vgi-bridge` in [[verifiable-git-infrastructure|VGI]], which relays `git-ns/bridge` events and jobs over TSP; and `vta-mcp`, where every AI tool call becomes a Trust Task classified by its declared risk class.

## Relation to TSP, DIDComm and REST

The layers are distinct and the ecosystem is careful to keep them so. TSP and DIDComm provide confidentiality, sender authentication and (for TSP) routing privacy; a TSP relationship is *admission*; the ACL is *authorization*; the Trust Task is the *statement of work and its evidence*. Consumer requirement 10 says validation of identity or proof is never itself authorization. The three carriers are chosen in the order TSP > DIDComm > HTTPS from what the peer advertises, and on every one of them the same document with the same proof is required — a binding "MUST NOT weaken the document requirements on the basis of a property of the transport." What remains of REST is the HTTPS *binding*: `POST /trust-tasks` carrying a signed document, not a route per verb.

## Status and Open Questions

The framework is Working Draft 0.7.0, versioned on its own SemVer line and not yet a ToIP Approved Deliverable; a normative conformance suite is promised before it leaves draft, with the registry's envelope schemas and generated runtimes as its basis. The specification itself notes the privacy cost of its own design — nearly every document names both parties in the clear and survives delivery — and treats identifier correlation as a live consideration, which is where [[correlation-scope]] and pairwise DIDs do their work. The VTI specification still records 49 tasks whose proof requirement was historically served behind a bearer JWT; closing that divergence is what the signed-door and REST-retirement work was for, and its remaining gaps are tracked in `vtc-trust-task-proof-enforcement.md`. Private, unpublished specifications are a conforming path (any authority but `trusttasks.org`, any slug but `trust-task*`), so the registry's public families are a floor, not a boundary.
